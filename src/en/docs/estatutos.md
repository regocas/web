## Chapter I. General provisions

### Article 1. Name, nature and legal personality

Under the name "Fundación Martina", or such other name as may ultimately be admitted by the competent Registry, a foundation of general interest is established, of a permanent nature and without profit-making purpose.

The Foundation shall be governed by the will of its founders as expressed in the Founding Charter and in the deed of incorporation, by these Articles, by the legislation on foundations of Galician interest and other applicable regulations, as well as by its internal good-governance rules validly approved.

The Foundation shall acquire legal personality upon its registration with the competent Foundations Registry and shall thereafter enjoy full legal capacity and capacity to act for the fulfilment of its founding purposes.

### Article 2. Founding Charter

The Founding Charter forms part of the constitutive act of the Foundation and expresses the founders' will to permanently commit a patrimonial endowment to the fulfilment of purposes of general interest linked to medical, biomedical, genetic, genomic, clinical and healthcare research, including the study of DNA and its variants and mutations, in rare diseases, especially ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders and related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions.

These Articles must be interpreted in accordance with the Founding Charter, as an expression of the institutional, scientific, healthcare, welfare and social purpose that inspires the creation of the Foundation, with particular attention to promoting biomedical, genetic, genomic and healthcare research into ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders and related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions.

### Article 3. Registered office

The Foundation's registered office is established at Calle Río Ulla, 10, 15179 Oleiros, province of A Coruña, Autonomous Community of Galicia.

The Board of Trustees may agree to change the registered office within the same municipality. Where the change exceeds that scope or entails an amendment to the Articles, the requirements, notifications and registrations that are legally required shall be observed.

### Article 4. Territorial scope of activity

The Foundation shall principally carry out its activities in Galicia, maintaining its registered office and its main centre of foundational activity within that territory, without prejudice to its ability, for the better fulfilment of its purposes, to promote or participate in actions, projects, agreements, scientific networks, welfare programmes, outreach campaigns or collaborations at the national, European or international level.

The national or international projection of certain actions shall be instrumental to the Foundation's purposes of general interest and shall be especially appropriate in relation to biomedical research, rare diseases, ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders, genetics, diagnosis, advanced therapies, patient registries or collaboration with specialised scientific centres.

### Article 5. Duration

The Foundation is established for an indefinite period, without prejudice to the grounds for dissolution provided for by law or under these Articles.

## Chapter II. Purpose, founding aims, activities and beneficiaries

### Article 6. Purpose and founding aims

1. The purpose and aims of general interest of the Foundation are to drive, promote, finance and, where appropriate, carry out activities aimed at medical, biomedical, genetic and genomic research related to the study of DNA, its variants and mutations, and to ataxias, including spastic ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases and related neurological or neurodegenerative disorders, as well as to improving scientific, genetic, healthcare and social knowledge of these conditions.

2. In order to fulfil this purpose and these aims, the Foundation may develop, promote, finance, support or carry out, directly or through collaboration with third parties, the following lines of action:

   - The promotion and funding of scientific, clinical, translational and epidemiological research into ataxias, rare diseases and movement disorders, including genetic and genomic research, the study of DNA and its variants and mutations, molecular diagnostics, biomarkers, therapies and clinical trials, through the award of grants, scholarships, contracts or other support instruments.
   - Collaboration and the execution of agreements with universities, hospitals, research centres, and public or private, national or international institutions, for the carrying out of research projects and programmes, data exchange, and the development of scientific infrastructure such as biobanks, registries or platforms, particularly when oriented toward ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders or related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions.
   - Scientific outreach and social awareness-raising through the organisation and funding of conferences, publications, educational materials and awareness campaigns, fostering knowledge, prevention and the fight against misinformation.
   - Support for initiatives to improve diagnosis and access to research, particularly in rare conditions, ataxias and movement disorders, including funding for multicentre studies, patient registries, assessment tools or similar actions, always in compliance with data-protection, bioethics and biomedical-research regulations.
   - The promotion of the participation of patients and families in research programmes and informational activities, particularly in the field of ataxias, movement disorders and rare diseases, fostering association-building, responsible support, and dialogue with the scientific and healthcare community.
   - Fundraising and patronage through donations, bequests, sponsorships, agreements, grants, corporate collaborations, earmarked funds, and any other lawful forms of funding intended for the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.

3. All activities shall be carried out on a non-profit basis, with resources and results applied to the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes in the manner legally provided and subject to applicable regulations.

### Article 7. Activities for the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes

In order to fulfil its purposes, the Foundation may carry out, fund, promote, collaborate in or participate in the following activities, among others:

- Announcing, awarding and managing grants, scholarships, subsidies, prizes, contracts or research programmes in ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases, genetics, genomics, the study of DNA and its mutations, molecular diagnostics, biomarkers, therapies, clinical trials, observational studies, translational research and personalised medicine.
- Promoting, funding or carrying out its own projects or projects in collaboration with hospitals, universities, health research institutes, scientific centres, biobanks, scientific societies, public administrations, private entities, patient associations and national or international organisations.
- Driving DNA, genetic, genomic, clinical, epidemiological, diagnostic or therapeutic studies aimed at improving knowledge, diagnosis, follow-up, treatment and quality of life for affected people, with particular attention to ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders and related molecular, genetic, biological or clinical mechanisms.
- Supporting the creation, maintenance or funding of registries, repositories, data platforms, biobanks, research networks or scientific infrastructure, including those aimed at the study of ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders and related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions, with full compliance with applicable regulations on data protection, biomedical research, bioethics, informed consent and confidentiality.
- Organising, promoting or funding conferences, congresses, seminars, publications, scientific materials, training activities, awareness campaigns and rigorous outreach programmes on rare diseases, ataxias, movement disorders, genetics, neurology, diagnosis and health.
- Promoting the informed participation of patients and families in outreach, scientific or guidance activities, always respecting their dignity, privacy, autonomy, image, personal data and special situation of vulnerability.
- Facilitating, funding, channelling or promoting, directly where legally possible and, where appropriate, through agreements with authorised professionals, centres or entities, medical-healthcare, pharmacological, pharmaceutical, psychological, psychiatric, therapeutic, welfare and social support for affected people and their families, especially minors or people in situations of vulnerability affected by ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases or related conditions.
- Guiding families to facilitate access to specialised resources, healthcare professionals, follow-up programmes, support organisations, public or private aid, and appropriate welfare services.
- Raising resources, donations, bequests, grants, sponsorships, corporate collaborations, earmarked funds and any other lawful contributions for allocation to the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.
- Carrying out ancillary or instrumental economic activities, provided they are compatible with the Foundation's purposes, with its non-profit nature, and with applicable regulations.

Welfare or support activities of a healthcare, pharmacological, psychological or psychiatric nature shall be carried out in full compliance with applicable regulations, without substituting for the competences of public health services, authorised healthcare centres or competent professionals, and preferably through agreements or arrangements with duly qualified entities and professionals where necessary.

### Article 8. Beneficiaries

The following, among others, may be beneficiaries of the Foundation's activity, in accordance with the Foundation's purposes and the criteria approved by the Board of Trustees, provided they may be recipients of its activities, whether natural or legal persons:

- Patients affected by ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases or related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions.
- Affected minors and people in situations of special vulnerability due to illness, disability, dependency, age or family circumstances.
- Families, carers and people close to affected individuals, in relation to information, guidance, companionship, support and participation activities.
- Researchers, research groups, healthcare professionals, universities, hospitals, health research institutes, scientific centres, biobanks, scientific societies and specialised entities.
- Patient associations, social organisations, public administrations, private institutions, foundations and organisations that collaborate toward similar purposes.
- Society at large, in awareness-raising, outreach, training and scientific and healthcare knowledge-promotion activities.

### Article 9. Criteria for the selection of beneficiaries, projects and grants

The selection of beneficiaries, projects, grants, scholarships, agreements or programmes shall be carried out in accordance with objective, transparent, traceable criteria appropriate to the Foundation's purposes.

In matters of biomedical or healthcare research, particular attention shall be paid to scientific quality, relevance to ataxias, movement disorders and rare diseases, technical and budgetary feasibility, the soundness of the research team, potential diagnostic, clinical, therapeutic or social impact, bioethical compliance, data protection, the absence of conflicts of interest, and alignment with the Foundation's priorities.

Trustees, founders and related persons may not be principal beneficiaries of the Foundation, except in legally permitted cases and with the necessary safeguards to ensure the absence of conflicts of interest, transparency, objectivity and full alignment with the Foundation's purposes.

## Chapter III. Assets, endowment and financial regime

### Article 10. Founding endowment

The Foundation's initial endowment amounts to 30,000 euros, contributed in the manner recorded in the deed of incorporation, and is permanently committed to the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.

The endowment must be adequate and sufficient for the initial fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes, being presumed sufficient when it reaches the legally established amount, and its adequacy must be justified when it is lower or when the nature of the contribution so requires, without prejudice to its subsequent increase through new contributions, donations, bequests, grants, returns, capitalisation of results, or any other lawfully acquired assets or rights.

Where the endowment consists of assets or rights other than money, the valuation and documentation legally required shall accompany it.

### Article 11. Assets of the Foundation

The Foundation's assets shall comprise:

- The initial founding endowment and its subsequent increases.
- The assets, rights and resources it acquires by any lawful title.
- Donations, inheritances, bequests, grants, sponsorships, agreements, earmarked funds or contributions accepted by the Board of Trustees.
- The returns, fruits, income and revenue derived from its assets, activities, collaborations or own resources.
- The intellectual and industrial property rights, data, materials, publications, tools or results that may belong to it under funded agreements or projects, always within the applicable legal framework.

### Article 12. Application of assets and allocation of income and revenue

The Foundation's assets, income, revenue and resources shall be committed to the fulfilment of its purposes of general interest.

The Foundation shall effectively allocate to the fulfilment of its founding purposes at least seventy percent of the net income and revenue obtained, within the terms and timeframes provided for in applicable legislation. The remainder must be allocated to increasing the founding endowment or reserves, once the legally admissible administration expenses have been deducted.

Administration, structural, communication, fundraising and representation expenses must be reasonable, proportionate to the size of the Foundation, duly justified, and aimed at maximising the impact on biomedical research, diagnosis, scientific outreach, support for patients and families, and the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.

The Board of Trustees shall ensure that the application of resources is effective, efficient, traceable and consistent with the Founding Charter, these Articles and applicable regulations, especially in relation to earmarked donations, grants, scientific agreements, multi-year projects, research programmes, biobanks, patient registries, or actions affecting minors, health data or genetic data.

### Article 13. Acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests and earmarked funds

The acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests, grants, agreements or earmarked funds shall be the responsibility of the Board of Trustees, in accordance with the Articles, the Board of Trustees' Regulations, the Code of Good Governance, and any internal policies that may be approved.

Contributions shall not be accepted where they compromise the Foundation's scientific or institutional independence, impose conditions incompatible with its purposes, seek to predetermine scientific results or beneficiaries without proper evaluation, are of unlawful or insufficiently established origin, or may give rise to significant reputational risk.

Donations or earmarked funds must be sufficiently documented and allow the monitoring of their application, without prejudice to legitimate confidentiality and to data-protection regulations.

### Article 14. Economic activities

The Foundation may carry out ancillary, complementary or instrumental economic activities, provided they are related to its purposes or necessary for its funding and sustainability, in accordance with applicable regulations.

Under no circumstances shall profits, surpluses or assets be distributed among founders, trustees, donors, collaborators or related persons. Any positive result shall be allocated to the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes or to strengthening its assets, within the legally permitted terms.

### Article 15. Financial year, accounting, accounts and audit

The financial year shall coincide with the calendar year, unless the Board of Trustees agrees otherwise within legally permitted terms.

The Foundation shall keep orderly accounts appropriate to its activity, with the legally required books and, in particular, a daybook and a book of inventories and annual accounts where applicable, kept separately where necessary for specific projects, earmarked donations, grants or agreements.

The Board of Trustees shall prepare and approve the annual accounts, the financial report, the activity report and the action plan within the legally established terms, approving the accounts within the legal period following the close of the financial year, submitting them to the Protectorate within the legally established period following their approval, and submitting the action plan for the following financial year within the legally established period.

An audit of accounts shall be carried out when legally mandatory or when the Board of Trustees voluntarily agrees to it for reasons of transparency, volume of resources, the nature of donors, the complexity of projects, or institutional expediency.

## Chapter IV. Governance of the Foundation: the Board of Trustees

### Article 16. Nature and powers of the Board of Trustees

The Board of Trustees is the governing, representative and administrative body of the Foundation. It is the responsibility of the Board of Trustees to fulfil the Foundation's purposes, to administer with diligence the assets and rights comprising the Foundation's patrimony, and to ensure the correct application of its resources.

The Board of Trustees shall always act in the interest of the Foundation and of its biomedical, genetic, genomic, healthcare, welfare and general-interest purposes, without subordination to personal, family, professional, economic, business, political or institutional interests unrelated to those purposes.

### Article 17. Composition

The Board of Trustees shall be composed of a minimum of three members and of the maximum number that the founders or the Board of Trustees itself may determine, within the legally admissible limits.

The Board of Trustees shall have, at least, a Chair and a Secretary, and may also have a Vice-Chair, Deputy Secretary, and other members.

Both natural and legal persons may be trustees. Legal persons shall designate a natural person as their representative in accordance with legally established terms.

The composition of the Board of Trustees shall seek to combine closeness to the founding will, institutional soundness, independence of judgement, and profiles suited to the fulfilment of the Foundation's scientific, healthcare, economic, legal, welfare and fundraising purposes, seeking to include or consult profiles with knowledge of rare diseases, neurology, neuropaediatrics, genetics, biomedical research or scientific-project management as the Foundation's development warrants.

### Article 17 bis. Chair

The Chair of the Board of Trustees shall hold the ordinary institutional representation of the Foundation and shall exercise the functions assigned by law, these Articles, the Board of Trustees' Regulations, and the resolutions validly adopted by the Board of Trustees.

The Chair shall, in particular:

- Call meetings of the Board of Trustees.
- Chair them, and order and direct their deliberations.
- Grant or withdraw permission to speak in accordance with criteria of order and proper functioning.
- Resolve ties by casting vote, unless otherwise provided by law or these Articles.
- Execute or order the execution of the Board of Trustees' resolutions.
- Sign the public or private documents necessary for their execution.
- Institutionally represent the Foundation before natural or legal persons, entities, authorities, bodies, public administrations and, where appropriate, judicial or administrative bodies.

The Chair shall always act within the framework of the Board of Trustees' resolutions and may not substitute for the Board's non-delegable powers, nor for the authorisations, notifications or controls that fall to the Protectorate, the Foundations Registry, or any competent authorities.

### Article 17 ter. Vice-Chair

The Board of Trustees may designate one or several Vice-Chairs, numbered ordinally where there is more than one, from among its members and by the procedure provided for in these Articles or in the Board of Trustees' Regulations.

The Vice-Chair shall, in order, replace the Chair in cases of vacancy, absence, illness, incapacity, abstention due to conflict of interest, or express delegation, and shall exercise such functions as may be entrusted by the Board of Trustees or by the Chair within the statutory framework.

While the office of Chair is vacant, and until the Board of Trustees designates the person to fill it, the relevant Vice-Chair shall assume its functions on an interim basis, without prejudice to the obligation to convene the Board of Trustees where necessary to fill the vacancy.

### Article 17 quater. Secretary and Deputy Secretary

The office of Secretary may be held by a trustee or a non-trustee. In the latter case, the Secretary shall have a voice but no vote, unless applicable regulations require otherwise.

The Secretary shall be responsible for safeguarding the Foundation's documentation, preparing and preserving the minutes of the Board of Trustees, issuing certifications with the Chair's approval, keeping the minute book and other corporate documentation, and assisting the Chair in the proper calling, documentation and formalisation of resolutions.

The Board of Trustees may designate a Deputy Secretary, whether a trustee or not, to support the Secretary and replace them in cases of vacancy, absence, illness, incapacity, or express delegation.

### Article 18. Acceptance of office

The office of trustee must be expressly accepted in the legally established manner and, where appropriate, notified to or registered with the competent Foundations Registry.

Acceptance of office shall entail the obligation to act with diligence, loyalty, independence, confidentiality, transparency, absence of conflict of interest, and full commitment to the Foundation's purposes.

### Article 19. Term, renewal and removal

The term of office of trustees shall be four years, without prejudice to the possibility of renewal, or indefinite if so validly established in the deed of incorporation or in these Articles.

Trustees shall cease to hold office on the legally established grounds, through resignation, death, incapacity, dissolution of the legal person, expiry of their term, removal, serious breach of their duties, or resolution adopted in accordance with applicable regulations and these Articles.

Removal from office must be documented and, where appropriate, notified to or registered with the competent Foundations Registry.

### Article 20. Gratuitousness of office, reimbursement of expenses, and contracting

Trustees shall discharge their office without remuneration, receiving no payment for the exercise of their functions as trustees.

They shall be entitled to reimbursement of duly justified expenses incurred in the discharge of their office, in accordance with criteria of austerity, proportionality, transparency and traceability.

The Foundation may only contract with trustees or related persons in legally permitted cases and under legally permitted conditions, with express approval, justification of need, market-consistent terms, absence of preferential treatment, and compliance with any required authorisations, notifications or accountability procedures, with documentary record kept of the conflict of interest, the abstention of the affected person, and the objective advantage or necessity for the Foundation.

### Article 21. Powers of the Board of Trustees

In addition to the powers conferred by law and these Articles, the Board of Trustees shall exercise the following functions:

- Ensuring the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.
- Approving the Foundation's overall strategy and its scientific, welfare and institutional priorities.
- Approving the annual action plan, internal budget, annual accounts, financial report and activity report.
- Approving calls for grants, scholarships, research contracts, agreements, scientific projects and welfare programmes linked to the Foundation's purposes.
- Approving the investment policy, asset management, and the acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests and earmarked funds.
- Appointing and removing members of the Scientific Advisory Committee and approving its Regulations.
- Approving the Board of Trustees' Regulations, the Code of Good Governance, and any other necessary internal policies.
- Resolving significant conflicts of interest and authorising, where appropriate, related-party transactions.
- Creating committees, working groups or advisory bodies.
- Amending the Articles where appropriate.
- Agreeing to the merger, dissolution and liquidation of the Foundation in the legally established terms.
- Ensuring compliance with data-protection, biomedical-research, bioethics, transparency, tax, accounting and other applicable regulations.
- Administering, acquiring, preserving, encumbering, disposing of and transferring the Foundation's assets and rights, subject to applicable regulations, to the Foundation's purposes, and to any authorisations or notifications that may be required.
- Exercising overall inspection, oversight and guidance of the Foundation's activity, approving its management plans and periodic action programmes, and determining the general criteria for the distribution and application of available funds.
- Appointing, dismissing and, where appropriate, setting the terms of the Foundation's management, technical, administrative or collaborating staff, and granting and revoking general or special powers of attorney.
- Appearing and acting on the Foundation's behalf before all manner of persons, entities, authorities, bodies, public administrations, courts and tribunals, being able to make requests, claims, appeals, actions, objections, withdrawals, settlements or submissions to arbitration, directly or through representatives, lawyers or attorneys.
- Carrying out the economic, financial, banking, contractual and documentary transactions necessary or convenient for the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes, including opening and closing accounts, collections, payments, deposits, investments, and the signing of public or private documents.

The foregoing powers are illustrative in nature and shall be exercised without prejudice to the competences legally attributed to the Protectorate, the Foundations Registry, and any competent authorities.

### Article 22. Meetings, notice, and prior documentation

The Board of Trustees shall meet at least twice a year, and in any case as often as necessary to approve accounts, the action plan, budgets, significant projects, and scientific or welfare follow-up decisions, and whenever convened by the Chair, requested by the number of trustees provided for by law or these Articles, or necessary for the proper management of the Foundation.

Notice shall be given in writing, including by email or any other means allowing proof of dispatch, with a minimum of ten days' notice, except in duly justified cases of urgency.

The notice shall include the agenda, the venue or connection method, the date, the time, and the documentation necessary to deliberate and adopt resolutions with sufficient knowledge of the matters concerned.

No prior notice shall be required, and the Board of Trustees shall be validly constituted to deal with any matter, when all its members are present or represented and unanimously agree to hold the meeting and to the corresponding agenda.

Meetings of the Board of Trustees may be held at the Foundation's registered office or at any other location, within or outside Galicia, when so indicated in the notice or agreed by all attending trustees.

When a decision concerns scientific projects, research grants, biomedical agreements, healthcare or pharmacological assistance, genetic data, genomic data, information on DNA and mutations, biological samples, patient registries or significant ethical questions, particularly in projects linked to ataxias, rare diseases, minors or vulnerable people, efforts shall be made to obtain a report from the Scientific Advisory Committee or sufficient independent technical opinion.

### Article 23. In-person, remote or hybrid meetings

The Board of Trustees may meet in person, by remote means, or in hybrid format, provided that the identity of attendees, the continuity of communication, the possibility of deliberation, and the documentary record of resolutions adopted are guaranteed.

### Article 24. Constitution, deliberation and adoption of resolutions

The Board of Trustees shall be validly constituted when a majority of its members, present or represented, attend the meeting, unless the law, these Articles or the Board of Trustees' Regulations require a reinforced quorum for particular matters.

Trustees may grant their representation to another trustee for a specific meeting, in writing and with specific instructions where they consider it appropriate. The Secretary shall keep a record of representations granted.

The Chair shall order the deliberation and shall seek to ensure that all trustees have a reasonable opportunity to participate. On scientific, technical, legal, economic, healthcare or particularly complex matters, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, external experts, legal advisers, auditors, project leads, or persons invited by reason of the subject matter may attend with a voice but no vote.

Resolutions shall be adopted by simple majority of the attending trustees, present or represented, unless the law, these Articles or the Board of Trustees' Regulations require a qualified majority for particular matters. The votes of those who must abstain due to conflict of interest shall not be counted as votes cast.

In the event of a tie, the Chair's vote shall be decisive, unless the law, these Articles or the Board of Trustees' Regulations establish another rule for the specific case.

Trustees affected by a real, potential or apparent conflict of interest must disclose it immediately and abstain from taking part in the deliberation, voting, execution or management of the affected matter, except for purely informational involvement authorised and documented by the Board of Trustees.

The Secretary shall record in the minutes those attending, representations, the essential deliberations, resolutions adopted, the outcome of votes, abstentions, conflicts of interest and, where applicable, dissenting votes.

### Article 24 bis. Reinforced majorities and non-delegable powers

Resolutions concerning the following matters shall require the favourable vote of at least two thirds of the members of the Board of Trustees, unless a higher majority is required by law:

- Amendment of the Articles.
- Approval or amendment of the Board of Trustees' Regulations, the Code of Good Governance, the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations, and the essential documents of the internal regulatory system.
- Appointment or removal of members of the Scientific Advisory Committee.
- Acceptance of donations, bequests, sponsorships or conditional earmarked funds of significant amount or with potential reputational impact.
- Approval of multi-year research projects or significant financial commitments.
- Related-party transactions with trustees, founders, family members, related entities, significant funders, or persons subject to a conflict of interest.
- Disposal, encumbrance or transfer of assets or rights forming part of the founding endowment or of particular relevance to the Foundation's purposes.
- Granting of general powers of attorney.
- Merger, demerger, dissolution, liquidation, or the destination of the resulting assets.

Under no circumstances shall the powers relating to the approval of the annual accounts, approval of the action plan, amendment of the Articles, merger, dissolution and liquidation be delegable, nor those requiring authorisation from the Protectorate or which are non-delegable under applicable regulations.

The Board of Trustees may delegate other powers or grant powers of attorney within legally permitted terms. Any delegation or power of attorney must be documented, specifying the powers, limits, duration and accountability arrangements, and must be notified or registered where required.

### Article 25. Minutes and documentation of resolutions

Minutes of each meeting shall be drawn up by the Secretary, with the Chair's approval. The minutes shall record, at least, the date, venue or means of the meeting, attendees, agenda, documentation examined, resolutions adopted, the outcome of votes, abstentions due to conflicts of interest and, where applicable, dissenting votes.

Minutes may be approved at the same session or at the following one. The Secretary shall safeguard the minute book and the documentary evidence of resolutions.

If the Board of Trustees does not expressly designate the person or persons responsible for executing a resolution, its execution shall be understood to fall to the Chair, without prejudice to any delegations or powers of attorney that may be granted.

The Secretary and, where applicable, the Deputy Secretary, even though they may not hold the status of trustee, shall be empowered to certify resolutions and to raise them to public instrument when necessary or convenient for their validity, registration or execution.

## Chapter V. Advisory bodies, good governance, and internal regulatory system

### Article 26. Scientific Advisory Committee

The Foundation shall have a Scientific Advisory Committee as a collegiate, technical, consultative and independent body, intended to bring scientific soundness, technical autonomy and traceability to the scientific, biomedical, genetic, clinical, diagnostic, outreach or research-funding decisions adopted by the Board of Trustees, particularly in relation to ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders and related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions.

The Scientific Advisory Committee shall be organically dependent on the Board of Trustees, which shall appoint and remove its members, but shall act with technical autonomy in the evaluation of projects, the formulation of recommendations, the assessment of scientific priorities, and the issuance of reports.

The Committee shall be composed of a minimum of three and a maximum of seven members, appointed from among persons of recognised standing in neurology, neuropaediatrics, genetics, biomedicine, bioinformatics, clinical research, molecular diagnostics, rare diseases, advanced therapies, clinical trials, epidemiology, bioethics, hereditary ataxias, movement disorders, neurogenetics, molecular biology, or lines of research related to ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders or other disciplines linked to the Foundation's purposes.

Its reports shall be non-binding, unless the Board of Trustees agrees otherwise for a specific call or project, but must be given special consideration in decisions of a scientific or biomedical nature. Where the Board of Trustees departs from a significant scientific recommendation, it must record its reasons in the corresponding minutes.

The composition, appointment, term, removal, operation, confidentiality, conflicts of interest, reports, project evaluation, and relationship with external experts shall be regulated in the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations approved by the Board of Trustees.

### Article 27. Basic functions of the Scientific Advisory Committee

The Scientific Advisory Committee shall, under the terms of its Regulations, be responsible for, among other functions:

- Advising the Board of Trustees on the Foundation's scientific strategy.
- Proposing priority lines of research into ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases, genetics, genomics, the study of DNA and its mutations, molecular diagnostics, biomarkers, therapies, clinical trials, observational studies, and personalised medicine.
- Evaluating research projects seeking funding or support from the Foundation.
- Assessing calls for grants, scholarships, contracts, scientific agreements, and research programmes.
- Issuing recommendations on scientific quality, feasibility, impact, budget, follow-up, bioethics, data protection, and expected results of projects, including specific assessment of risks, expectations and safeguards where minors, genetic data, genomic data, information on DNA and mutations, biological samples, or particularly vulnerable patients are involved.
- Proposing the participation of external experts where the specialty of the project so requires.

### Article 28. Other bodies, committees, or working groups

The Board of Trustees may create legal, economic, welfare, communications, fundraising, compliance, ethics or other committees, working groups or advisory bodies that it considers necessary for the better fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.

The creation, modification or abolition of standing bodies shall be carried out in accordance with applicable regulations, these Articles and, where appropriate, the notifications, authorisations or registrations required before the Protectorate or the competent Registry.

### Article 29. Code of Good Governance

The Foundation shall have a Code of Good Governance approved by the Board of Trustees, applicable, as appropriate, to trustees, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, executives, employees, collaborators, volunteers, external advisers, evaluators, suppliers, donors, sponsors and collaborating entities that take a significant part in the Foundation's activity.

The Code of Good Governance shall develop the principles of general interest, non-profit nature, scientific independence, transparency, accountability, integrity, prevention of conflicts of interest, efficient use of resources, enhanced protection of health data, genetic data, genomic data, DNA and mutations, respect for patients and families, responsible scientific communication, and regulatory compliance.

### Article 30. Board of Trustees' Regulations

The internal functioning of the Board of Trustees shall be developed through Board of Trustees' Regulations, approved by the governing body itself, which shall regulate notice of meetings, prior documentation, deliberation, adoption of resolutions, minutes, execution of decisions, delegations, conflicts of interest, related-party transactions, acceptance of donations, the relationship with the Scientific Advisory Committee, transparency, financial information, and monitoring of the Foundation's governance.

### Article 31. Internal regulatory system

The Founding Charter, these Articles, the Board of Trustees' Regulations, the Code of Good Governance, the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations, and the internal policies approved from time to time by the Board of Trustees make up the Foundation's internal regulatory system.

All of these documents must be interpreted in a coordinated manner, in accordance with the Foundation's purposes and the principles of transparency, scientific independence, non-profit nature, efficient application of resources, data protection, bioethics, institutional integrity, protection of patients and families, and respect for the general interest.

In the event of contradiction, applicable legislation and these Articles shall prevail, without prejudice to the obligation to adapt the remaining internal documents accordingly.

## Chapter VI. Transparency, integrity, data protection and bioethics

### Article 32. Transparency and accountability

The Foundation shall publish or make available, in a manner proportionate to its size and legal obligations, information relating to its Articles, the composition of the Board of Trustees, the composition of the Scientific Advisory Committee where applicable, the Code of Good Governance, the annual activity report, the annual accounts, the main projects funded, the general criteria for awarding grants, and aggregate data on donations, sponsorships and earmarked funds, respecting legitimate confidentiality and data-protection regulations.

The annual report shall seek to reflect the activities carried out, resources applied, projects funded, results achieved, impact indicators, and the degree of fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.

### Article 33. Conflicts of interest and related-party transactions

Trustees, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, and other persons subject to the internal regulatory system must avoid situations in which their personal, family, professional, scientific, economic or institutional interests could interfere with the interests of the Foundation.

Where a real, potential or apparent conflict exists, it must be disclosed immediately, and the affected person shall abstain from taking part in the corresponding deliberation, evaluation, decision or management, except for purely informational involvement authorised and documented.

The Foundation shall maintain an internal register of declarations of interests, related-party transactions, abstentions, and significant decisions, under the terms established by the Code of Good Governance, the Board of Trustees' Regulations, and the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations.

### Article 34. Data protection, confidentiality, and use of image

The Foundation shall adopt enhanced safeguards when it processes, funds or facilitates the processing of data relating to health, genetics, diagnosis, family members, patients, minors, disease registries, biological samples, biobanks, clinical studies, testimonials, images, or otherwise especially sensitive information, including genetic data, genomic data, and information on DNA, genetic variants, mutations and molecular findings not confined to a specific gene, as well as identifying information about minors or family members.

The principles of lawfulness, fairness, transparency, data minimisation, purpose limitation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity, confidentiality and proactive accountability shall apply, among others.

When the Foundation collects testimonials, images, videos, medical records, family data, or information concerning minors or patients, it must obtain the necessary authorisations and consents, use comprehensible language, and limit public exposure to what is strictly necessary for the legitimate purpose pursued.

### Article 35. Bioethics and responsible research

Projects funded, promoted or supported by the Foundation must have, where appropriate, the authorisations, ethics-committee opinions, informed consents, insurance, confidentiality safeguards, and regulatory requirements applicable, especially where they involve research with health data, genetic data, minors, biological samples, biobanks, clinical trials, or multicentre collaboration.

The Foundation shall not substitute for legally competent ethics committees, nor shall it assume healthcare, pharmacological, scientific or welfare authorisation functions that fall to authorities, centres, accredited committees, or qualified professionals.

All communication concerning research, DNA studies and their mutations, advances, trials, diagnoses, therapies or treatments must be prudent, truthful, comprehensible and scientifically responsible, avoiding the generation of unfounded therapeutic expectations, promises of a cure, or confusion between preliminary research and available treatment, with special caution in public communications relating to minors, rare diseases, ataxias, or lines of research at an early stage.

### Article 36. Relations with patients, families and minors

The participation of patients and families in the Foundation's activities shall take place with respect for their dignity, privacy, autonomy, image, personal data, and special situation of vulnerability.

Fundraising, communication or outreach campaigns must avoid the unnecessary exposure of minors, health data, genetic data, or identifiable information about patients or family members, ensuring that any reference to Martina or other minors preserves their dignity, privacy, image and consent within legally required terms.

The Foundation shall seek to ensure that its information, companionship or guidance activities do not generate dependency, care-related confusion, or expectations incompatible with the available scientific evidence.

## Chapter VII. Amendment, merger, dissolution and liquidation

### Article 37. Amendment of the Articles

The Board of Trustees may agree to amend these Articles when it is expedient for the better fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes, to adapt the Foundation to regulatory changes, or to improve its organisation and functioning.

Any amendment to the Articles shall comply with applicable regulations and shall require, where appropriate, notification, authorisation, approval or registration before the Protectorate and the competent Foundations Registry.

### Article 38. Merger

The Foundation may merge with one or more other non-profit entities where this results in the better fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes and the legally established requirements are met.

The merger agreement must preserve, to the extent legally possible, the scientific, biomedical, healthcare, welfare and general-interest purpose that inspires the Foundation, especially its orientation toward ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases, and responsible support for patients and families.

### Article 39. Dissolution

The Foundation shall be dissolved on the grounds provided for in applicable regulations, due to impossibility of fulfilling its purposes, by resolution of the Board of Trustees in legally established cases, or by final judicial or administrative ruling where applicable.

The resolution of dissolution must be adopted with the required majorities and procedures, and shall be subject to the notifications, authorisations or registrations that are legally applicable.

### Article 40. Liquidation and destination of assets

Once the Foundation is dissolved, it shall be liquidated by the Board of Trustees or by liquidators appointed in accordance with applicable regulations.

The resulting assets shall be allocated to non-profit entities pursuing similar purposes of general interest, preferably dedicated to biomedical, genetic, genomic or healthcare research, rare diseases, ataxias, movement disorders, neurodegenerative conditions, or welfare support for patients and families, and which are subject to a comparable tax regime where legally applicable.

The destination of the assets shall be determined by the Board of Trustees or by the liquidators, subject to the founding will, these Articles, applicable regulations, and the approval or oversight of the relevant Protectorate.

## Chapter VIII. Applicable regulations and interpretation

### Article 41. Applicable regulations

The Foundation shall be governed by the founding will expressed in the Founding Charter and in the deed of incorporation, by these Articles, by the legislation on foundations of Galician interest, by such basic or supplementary state regulations as may be applicable, by the tax regulations applicable to non-profit entities and patronage should the Foundation avail itself of that regime, and by the remaining civil, administrative, healthcare, biomedical, tax, accounting, data-protection, intellectual-property, transparency and compliance regulations applicable to its activity.

### Article 42. Interpretation

Any doubts as to the interpretation of these Articles shall be resolved with regard to the founding will, the best achievement of the purposes of general interest, the protection of scientific independence, transparency, the protection of patients and families, the Foundation's founding focus on the study of DNA and its variants and mutations, and on ataxias, movement disorders and rare diseases, and with regard to compliance with applicable regulations.

The Foundation's internal rules must be applied consistently with these Articles and with the Founding Charter. The Board of Trustees shall promote their review and updating where the Foundation's development, regulatory changes, recommendations of the Scientific Advisory Committee, operational experience, or the requirements of good governance so advise.

These Articles are approved by the founders and incorporated into the Foundation's deed of incorporation, without prejudice to any adaptations that may prove necessary at the request of the authorising Notary, the Protectorate, or the competent Foundations Registry.
