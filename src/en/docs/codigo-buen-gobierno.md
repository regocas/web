## Preamble

The Foundation carries out or funds activities of general interest in a particularly sensitive field: biomedical research, genetics, rare diseases, ataxias, diagnosis, scientific outreach, and relations with patients and families. Public trust does not depend solely on charitable or scientific purpose, but on how funds are governed, projects are selected, results are communicated, personal data are managed, and scientific independence is preserved.

This Code sets out the values, principles, and standards of conduct that must govern the actions of the Foundation and of the persons connected to it. It does not replace legal obligations but rather develops them and raises the internal standard of integrity, transparency, and accountability.

### Article 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Code of Good Governance is to establish the principles, values, and standards of conduct that must govern the organization, governance, management, and activity of the Foundation, ensuring that all of its resources are applied in a transparent, efficient, independent manner consistent with the Founding Charter, the Articles, and its research purposes in the biomedical, genetic, health, and general-interest fields, with particular attention to ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders associated with variants of the VPS13D gene, and related neurological or neurodegenerative rare diseases.

### Article 2. Subjective scope of application

This Code shall apply, as relevant, to members of the Board of Trustees, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, executives, employees, collaborators, volunteers, external advisors, expert evaluators, suppliers, partner entities, donors, and sponsors who play a significant role in the Foundation's activity.

### Article 3. Material scope

This Code shall apply to institutional, scientific, financial, contractual, and communications activity, fundraising, the granting of aid, relations with patients and families, data protection, use of image, public outreach, and collaboration with third parties.

### Article 4. Internal regulatory framework

The Founding Charter, the Articles, the Board of Trustees Regulations, this Code, the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations, and the internal policies approved by the Board of Trustees together make up the Foundation's internal regulatory framework. All of these shall be interpreted in a coordinated manner and in accordance with the Foundation's purposes, with applicable law and the Articles prevailing in the event of any contradiction.

### Article 5. Guiding principles

The Foundation shall act in accordance with the following principles:

- General interest and absence of profit motive.
- Scientific and institutional independence.
- Transparency and accountability.
- Integrity, loyalty, and prevention of conflicts of interest.
- Efficiency, restraint, and proportionality in the use of resources.
- Respect for the dignity, privacy, and autonomy of patients and families.
- Enhanced protection of health data, genetic data, and other special categories of data.
- Compliance with applicable regulations on biomedical research, clinical trials, bioethics, data protection, transparency, taxation, and anti-money-laundering rules, where applicable.
- Truthful, prudent, and scientifically responsible public communication.

### Article 6. Foundational interest and absence of private benefit

No person connected to the Foundation may use their position to obtain personal, family, professional, financial, or reputational advantages incompatible with the Foundation's purposes.

Any surplus, returns, or income the Foundation may obtain shall be applied in full to the fulfillment of its purposes, with no direct or indirect distribution among trustees, founders, donors, employees, or connected persons, except for legitimate, justified, and legally permissible consideration for services rendered.

### Article 7. Scientific independence

The Foundation shall preserve the technical and scientific independence of its decisions, particularly in the selection of projects, grants, scholarships, agreements, publications, research programs, institutional alliances, and the communication of results.

Donors, sponsors, or collaborators may not unduly influence the Foundation's scientific criteria, the independent evaluation of projects, or the content of scientific reports or communications.

### Article 8. Scientific integrity

The Foundation shall promote standards of scientific integrity, data accuracy, traceability of results, respect for authorship, prevention of plagiarism, proper preservation of scientific documentation, and responsible communication of advances, limitations, and uncertainties. In projects related to VPS13D, ataxias, movement disorders, genetic data, biological samples, biobanks, or clinical trials, the Foundation shall seek to obtain an evaluation or recommendation from the Scientific Advisory Committee or from suitable independent experts.

### Article 9. Bioethics and protection of individuals

Projects funded, promoted, or supported by the Foundation must hold, where applicable, the necessary authorizations, ethics committee opinions, informed consents, insurance, confidentiality guarantees, and other required regulatory requirements.

The Foundation shall not substitute for legally competent ethics committees, nor shall it assume health or scientific authorization functions that fall to accredited authorities, centers, or committees.

Where the funded or promoted activity involves biological samples, biobanks, patient registries, genetic data, or particularly sensitive health data, the Foundation shall require the responsible center to demonstrate sufficient informed consent, ethical or regulatory authorization where applicable, a regime governing access to samples or data, traceability, and confidentiality guarantees.

### Article 10. Health data and genetic data

The Foundation shall adopt enhanced safeguards when it processes, funds, or facilitates the processing of data relating to health, genetics, diagnosis, family members, patients, minors, disease registries, biological samples, biobanks, or clinical studies.

Among other principles, the Foundation shall apply lawfulness, fairness, transparency, data minimization, purpose limitation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity, confidentiality, and proactive accountability.

Before promoting or funding the processing of health data, genetic data, or data concerning minors, the Foundation shall, where applicable, document the position it assumes with respect to data protection — controller, joint controller, processor, funder, or mere collaborator — and shall require the necessary contracts, joint-controllership agreements, impact assessments, security measures, and authorizations.

### Article 11. Consent, information, and use of image

Where the Foundation collects testimonials, images, videos, medical records, family data, or information about minors or patients, it shall obtain the appropriate authorizations and consents, use clear and understandable language, and limit public exposure to what is strictly necessary for the legitimate purpose pursued.

### Article 12. Relations with patients and families

The Foundation may promote the participation of patients and families in informational, outreach, social, or scientific activities, always guaranteeing respect for their dignity, privacy, autonomy, situation of vulnerability, and their right not to be used instrumentally for fundraising or communication purposes.

### Article 13. Responsible scientific communication

The Foundation shall avoid creating unfounded therapeutic expectations, promises of a cure, misleading simplifications, or messages that confuse preliminary research with an available treatment.

All communication regarding research, advances, trials, diagnoses, or therapies must be prudent, truthful, understandable, and, where necessary, reviewed by persons with scientific or medical expertise.

### Article 14. Proactive transparency

The Foundation shall publish or make available, in a manner proportionate to its size and legal obligations, the following information:

- The Articles currently in force.
- The composition of the Board of Trustees and, where applicable, of the Scientific Advisory Committee.
- The Code of Good Governance.
- The annual activity report.
- The annual action plan, where legally required or advisable for reasons of transparency.
- The annual accounts and, where applicable, the audit report.
- The main projects funded or promoted, with an aggregate indication of their purpose, status of execution, and expected impact, particularly where they relate to VPS13D, ataxias, movement disorders, or related rare diseases.
- The general criteria for granting aid, scholarships, and subsidies.
- Aggregate information on donations, sponsorships, and earmarked funds, as well as on their general application, respecting legitimate confidentiality, data protection law, and commitments validly made to donors or beneficiaries.

### Article 15. Accountability

The Foundation shall render accounts to the competent Protectorate and Registry, to donors, collaborators, beneficiaries, and society at large, on the terms legally required and in accordance with voluntarily assumed commitments.

The annual report shall endeavor to include information on activities carried out, resources applied, projects funded, results achieved, impact indicators, and the degree to which the Foundation's purposes have been fulfilled.

### Article 16. Allocation of funds

The Foundation's resources shall be allocated primarily to the fulfillment of its founding purposes and, in any case, in the percentage and under the conditions required by applicable law. Structural, representation, communication, fundraising, and administrative expenses must be reasonable, proportionate, and geared toward maximizing direct impact on research, diagnosis, scientific outreach, patient support, and programs related to VPS13D, ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders, and related neurological or neurodegenerative rare diseases. Earmarked funds shall be applied in accordance with their accepted purpose, provided this is compatible with the Articles and the Founding Charter.

### Article 17. Donations, sponsorships, bequests, and earmarked funds

The acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests, or earmarked funds must respect the Foundation's purposes, its independence, applicable law, and the principles of transparency, reputation, traceability, and effective application of funds.

- Contributions of unlawful or insufficiently proven origin shall not be accepted.
- Contributions that would compromise the Foundation's scientific or institutional independence shall not be accepted.
- Conditions imposing preferential treatment, predetermined selection of beneficiaries without evaluation, or deviation from approved criteria shall not be accepted.
- Contributions whose publicity could cause serious reputational harm or is incompatible with the Foundation's values shall not be accepted.
- Earmarked donations must be documented in writing, identifying their purpose, conditions, time frame for application, and method of monitoring, and must allow for accounting and material traceability. Where the amount, origin, nature of the donor, or conditions of the contribution so advise, the Foundation shall carry out a reasonable check of lawfulness, reputation, absence of conflict of interest, and compatibility with scientific independence.

### Article 18. Relations with donors and sponsors

The Foundation shall treat its donors and sponsors with transparency, respect, and truthfulness. It shall inform them of how their contributions are used on the agreed terms, without granting them powers of scientific direction or undue control over projects, researchers, or results.

### Article 19. Fundraising

Fundraising campaigns must be truthful, proportionate, and respectful of patients and families, and must avoid emotionally abusive messages, unnecessary exposure of minors, or the use of health data without an adequate legal basis.

### Article 20. Conflicts of interest

Every person subject to this Code must avoid situations in which their personal, family, professional, scientific, financial, or institutional interests could interfere with the interests of the Foundation.

Where a real, potential, or apparent conflict exists, it must be reported immediately, and the person affected shall refrain from taking part in the relevant deliberation, evaluation, decision, or management, except for authorized and documented purely informational participation. Such abstention must, in significant matters, be recorded in the corresponding minutes or decision file.

### Article 21. Register of interests

The Foundation shall maintain an internal register of declarations of interest, related-party transactions, abstentions, and significant decisions of trustees, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, executives, external evaluators, and persons involved in significant decisions, in coordination with the Board of Trustees Regulations and the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations.

Declarations shall be updated at least annually and whenever a significant change occurs.

### Article 22. Gifts, invitations, and hospitality

No gifts, invitations, favors, or courtesies that could influence, or appear to influence, the independent judgment of the Foundation or of the persons acting on its behalf may be accepted.

Courtesies of symbolic value may be accepted, provided they are socially appropriate, proportionate, and not linked to a specific decision.

### Article 23. Procurement and suppliers

The procurement of goods or services must be carried out on the basis of need, quality, suitability, reasonable price, transparency, and the absence of conflicts of interest.

For significant contracts, the Foundation shall seek to obtain several offers or justify the choice of supplier, particularly where there is a relationship with trustees, donors, researchers, or connected persons.

### Article 24. Agreements with research centers, hospitals, and universities

Scientific collaboration agreements must define their purpose, duration, obligations, budget, ownership and use of results, publications, data protection, confidentiality, the regime governing intellectual and industrial property, ethical evaluation, use of biological samples or genetic data where applicable, rules on access to registries or biobanks, monitoring mechanisms, acknowledgment of funding, and any eventual social return on results.

### Article 25. Intellectual and industrial property

The Foundation shall seek to ensure that funded projects expressly regulate the ownership, use, exploitation, dissemination, and eventual social return of results, publications, databases, materials, developments, biomarkers, diagnostic tools, software, or inventions that may be generated.

### Article 26. Publications and acknowledgment of funding

Projects funded by the Foundation must appropriately acknowledge the funding received in publications, communications, materials, or results, without prejudice to respect for scientific independence, editorial standards, and the confidentiality of protected data.

### Article 27. Minors and vulnerable groups

Any activity affecting minors, persons with disabilities, patients, or vulnerable groups must be designed with special care, data minimization, sufficient authorization, understandable language, consent or assent where applicable, prior review of the use of image, and an approach of enhanced protection, avoiding their instrumental use in fundraising or communication campaigns.

### Article 28. Volunteering and collaborators

Volunteers and collaborators must receive clear information about their duties, limits, confidentiality obligations, data processing, and standards of conduct. The Foundation shall prevent volunteers from taking on technical, health-related, scientific, or data-management functions for which they are not qualified or authorized.

### Article 29. Use of the Foundation's resources and assets

The Foundation's resources, accounts, brand, materials, databases, digital channels, and other assets shall be used exclusively for the Foundation's purposes and in accordance with applicable internal authorizations.

### Article 30. Social media and digital presence

The Foundation's digital presence must respect the principles of truthfulness, scientific prudence, data protection, respect for patients and families, appropriate use of image, and consistency with the Foundation's purposes.

### Article 31. Channel for queries and incidents

The Foundation shall establish an internal channel, proportionate to its size, for raising queries, flagging risks, reporting conflicts of interest, related-party transactions, incidents concerning personal data, use of image, scientific communication, or the application of earmarked funds, and for reporting any incident relating to compliance with this Code.

Communications shall be handled with confidentiality, good faith, and a prohibition on retaliation against anyone who reports responsibly.

### Article 32. Regulatory compliance

The Foundation shall act in accordance with applicable law on foundations, the tax regime for non-profit entities, patronage, accounting, data protection, biomedical research, clinical trials, anti-money-laundering, intellectual property, procurement, transparency, and any other rules applicable to its activity.

### Article 33. Proactive accountability

Compliance with this Code shall not be limited to avoiding infringements; it shall also require documenting significant decisions, identifying risks, adopting preventive measures, periodically reviewing procedures, and retaining reasonable evidence of compliance.

### Article 34. Monitoring of the Code

The Board of Trustees shall be responsible for overseeing compliance with this Code. It may entrust its monitoring to the Secretariat, to a good-governance committee, or to a compliance officer, depending on the Foundation's development and size.

### Article 35. Consequences of non-compliance

Non-compliance with this Code may give rise, depending on the nature of the relationship, to a warning, a requirement to abstain, removal from office, termination of collaboration, referral to the Board of Trustees, contractual review, or the adoption of appropriate legal measures.

### Article 36. Training and dissemination

The Foundation shall seek to disseminate this Code among trustees, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, employees, collaborators, and other relevant third parties. Where the activity requires it, it shall organize basic training sessions on conflicts of interest, data protection, confidentiality, responsible communication, and the management of earmarked funds.

### Article 37. Periodic review

This Code shall be reviewed at least every three years, or sooner if warranted by regulatory changes, the Foundation's growth, practical experience, audit recommendations, amendments to the Founding Charter, the Articles, or the internal regulatory framework, the start of activities involving genetic data, biological samples, biobanks, or clinical trials, or significant incidents.

### Article 38. Acceptance

Persons subject to this Code must expressly accept it where their relationship with the Foundation so warrants. Acceptance may be recorded in the minutes accepting appointment to office, in a contract, an agreement, a letter of engagement, a declaration of interests, or a separate document.

### Article 39. Entry into force

This Code shall enter into force upon its approval by the Board of Trustees and shall remain in force until replaced or amended by express resolution.

The Code shall be interpreted in coordination with the Founding Charter and the Articles, preserving scientific priority in relation to VPS13D, the protection of patients and families, scientific independence, transparency, and the general interest.
