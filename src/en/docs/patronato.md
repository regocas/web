## Preamble

The Board of Trustees is the Foundation's governing, representative and administrative body. In a foundation oriented towards biomedical, genetic and health research, its actions must combine closeness to the founding purpose with institutional rigour, independence of judgement, traceability of decisions, control of conflicts of interest and reinforced protection of the trust placed in the Foundation by donors, researchers, patients and families.

These Regulations develop the internal operating rules of the Board of Trustees and must be interpreted in coordination with the Bylaws, the Code of Good Governance, the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations and the internal policies approved by the Foundation. Their purpose is to provide the Foundation with procedures that are clear, proportionate and compatible with the entity's future growth.

### Article 1. Purpose

These Regulations govern the organisation, operation, convening, deliberation, adoption of resolutions and documentation of the decisions of the Foundation's Board of Trustees, as well as the duties of its members, the conflict-of-interest regime and the relationship of the Board of Trustees with the advisory bodies and with the Foundation's internal policies.

### Article 2. Nature and institutional position of the Board of Trustees

The Board of Trustees is the Foundation's governing, representative and administrative body and shall exercise its powers subject to the law, the Bylaws, these Regulations, the Code of Good Governance and the other internal documents approved by the Foundation.

The Board of Trustees shall at all times act in the interest of the Foundation and of its biomedical, genetic, health and general-interest purposes, without subordination to personal, family, professional, economic, business, political or institutional interests unrelated to those purposes.

### Article 3. Principles of conduct

Members of the Board of Trustees must act in accordance with the principles of diligence, loyalty, independence of judgement, good faith, confidentiality, transparency, austerity, efficiency in the application of resources, prevention of conflicts of interest, scientific integrity and respect for the dignity of patients and families.

- Priority of the founding purpose over any particular interest.
- Effective application of resources to research, diagnosis, scientific dissemination and activities linked to the Foundation's purposes.
- Respect for the guarantees of data protection, bioethics, informed consent and confidentiality where an activity may affect health data, genetic data or biological samples.
- Traceability of significant decisions through minutes, reports and sufficient documentation.

### Article 4. Essential functions of the Board of Trustees

The Board of Trustees, in addition to the functions provided for in law and in the Bylaws, is responsible for the following essential functions:

- Approving the Foundation's general strategy and its scientific and institutional priorities.
- Approving the annual action plan, the internal budget, the annual accounts, the financial report and the activity report.
- Approving calls for grants, scholarships, research contracts, agreements and scientific programmes.
- Approving the policy on the acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests and restricted-purpose funds.
- Appointing and removing members of the Scientific Advisory Committee.
- Approving the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations and any amendments thereto.
- Resolving significant conflicts of interest and authorising, where applicable, related-party transactions.
- Resolving on amendments to the Bylaws, and on the merger, dissolution or winding-up of the Foundation, under the legally established terms.
- Ensuring that the Foundation's activity complies with the regulations on data protection, biomedical research, bioethics, transparency and regulatory compliance.

### Article 5. Composition and recommended profiles

The Board of Trustees shall be composed of the number of members provided for in the Bylaws. As far as possible, its composition shall seek a balanced presence of foundational, legal, financial, healthcare, scientific, institutional, communications and fundraising profiles, appropriate to the Foundation's biomedical and research nature.

The inclusion of scientific or healthcare profiles on the Board of Trustees shall not replace the independent technical function of the Scientific Advisory Committee, unless expressly agreed and always subject to the conflict-of-interest regime.

### Article 6. Acceptance, term, renewal and removal

Acceptance of the office of trustee shall be formalised in the manner legally established and shall entail the express acceptance of the Bylaws, of these Regulations and of the Code of Good Governance.

The term, renewal and removal of trustees shall be governed by the Bylaws and applicable legislation. Removal shall not release a trustee from the duties of confidentiality regarding information known during the exercise of the office.

### Article 7. Gratuitousness and reimbursement of expenses

The office of trustee shall be unpaid, without prejudice to the right to reimbursement of duly justified expenses incurred in the performance of the office, in accordance with criteria of reasonableness, austerity and transparency.

The Foundation may contract with trustees or related persons only where legally permissible, duly justified, carried out on market terms, and in compliance with the applicable regime of authorisation, abstention and documentation.

### Article 8. Duty of attendance, preparation and information

Trustees must attend meetings of the Board of Trustees duly prepared, examine the documentation provided, request any information they consider necessary and participate actively in the deliberation and oversight of the matters submitted for decision.

The Secretariat shall provide the documentation with sufficient notice, particularly regarding the approval of accounts, the activity report, scientific grants, research agreements, restricted-purpose donations, asset transactions, conflicts of interest and related-party transactions.

### Article 9. Notice of meeting

The Board of Trustees shall be convened in writing, including by email or another electronic means allowing a record to be kept, with at least ten calendar days' notice, except for duly justified reasons of urgency.

The notice of meeting shall include the agenda, the venue or connection system, the date, the time and the documentation necessary to deliberate and adopt resolutions with sufficient knowledge of the matters concerned.

### Article 10. Prior documentation

For matters of particular significance, an executive note shall be attached, where appropriate, identifying the matter, background, proposed decision, economic impact, legal, scientific or reputational risks, any conflict of interest and supporting documentation.

Where the decision concerns scientific or biomedical projects, efforts shall be made to include a report from the Scientific Advisory Committee or, at the very least, sufficient independent technical judgement.

### Article 11. In-person, remote or hybrid meetings

The Board of Trustees may meet in person, by remote means or in hybrid format, provided that the identity of those attending, the continuity of communication, the possibility of deliberation and the documentary record of the resolutions adopted are guaranteed.

The Secretariat may retain evidence of attendance, connection, voting and documentation sent, in compliance with data protection regulations.

### Article 12. Agenda

The agenda shall be set by the Chair with the assistance of the Secretariat. Any trustee may request the inclusion of additional items, giving reasons for their relevance to the Foundation, with sufficient notice for their incorporation and supporting documentation.

No resolutions may be adopted on matters not included in the agenda unless all trustees are present or represented and unanimously agree to address the matter.

### Article 13. Constitution of the Board of Trustees

The Board of Trustees shall be validly constituted when the quorum requirements set out in the Bylaws or in law are met. In the absence of a specific statutory rule, the attendance of a majority of its members, present or represented, shall be sought.

The Secretariat shall keep a record of those attending, proxies, apologies, guests and any abstentions due to conflicts of interest.

### Article 14. Deliberation

The Chair shall conduct the deliberation and shall ensure that all trustees have a reasonable opportunity to speak. On scientific, technical or particularly complex matters, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, external experts, legal advisers, auditors or project leads may be invited to attend with a right to speak but not to vote.

### Article 15. Adoption of resolutions

Resolutions shall be adopted by the majority provided for in the Bylaws or in law. In the absence of a specific provision, they shall be adopted by a simple majority of those attending, excluding from the vote those who must abstain due to a conflict of interest.

The Chair may promote the search for consensus on particularly sensitive matters, even where this is not legally required.

### Article 16. Reinforced majorities

Resolutions relating to the following matters shall require a reinforced majority of at least two-thirds of the members of the Board of Trustees, unless the Bylaws or the law require a higher majority:

- Amendment of the Bylaws.
- Approval or amendment of these Regulations.
- Approval or amendment of the Code of Good Governance.
- Approval or amendment of the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations.
- Appointment or removal of members of the Scientific Advisory Committee.
- Acceptance of donations, bequests or conditional sponsorships of significant amount or with reputational impact.
- Approval of multi-year research projects or significant financial commitments.
- Related-party transactions involving trustees, family members, related entities or significant donors.
- Merger, spin-off, dissolution or winding-up of the Foundation.
- Disposal or encumbrance of assets of particular significance or linked to the endowment or to the Foundation's purposes.

### Article 17. Dissenting vote and record in the minutes

Any trustee may request that the substance of their vote and, briefly, the essential reasons for their position be recorded in the minutes. Where a decision departs from a significant technical report of the Scientific Advisory Committee, it is recommended that the specific reasoning be placed on record.

### Article 18. Minutes

Minutes of each meeting shall be drawn up by the Secretariat, with the approval of the Chair. The minutes shall record, at least, the date, place or means of holding the meeting, those attending, the agenda, the documentation examined, the resolutions adopted, the results of votes, abstentions due to conflicts of interest and, where applicable, dissenting votes.

Minutes may be approved at the meeting itself or at the following one. The Secretariat shall keep custody of the minute book and the documentary evidence of the resolutions.

### Article 19. Implementation of resolutions

It shall fall to the Chair, the Secretariat, Management if one exists, or the person expressly empowered to do so, to implement the resolutions of the Board of Trustees on the terms approved.

Where a resolution requires execution before a notary, registration, notification to the Protectorate, administrative authorisation or the formalisation of a contract, the Secretariat shall follow up on the matter until it has been fully implemented.

### Article 20. Delegations and powers of attorney

The Board of Trustees may delegate powers or grant powers of attorney on the terms legally permitted and within the limitations set out in the Bylaws and in applicable regulations.

Every delegation or power of attorney must be recorded in writing, specifying the powers granted, the limits, the duration and the accountability regime. Powers that may not be delegated by law or under the Bylaws may not be delegated.

### Article 21. Relationship with the Scientific Advisory Committee

The Board of Trustees shall request a report from the Scientific Advisory Committee on decisions affecting scientific strategy, the granting of research funding, project evaluation, significant scientific agreements, biobanks, patient registries, genetic data, biological samples or participation in clinical trials or studies.

Reports of the Scientific Advisory Committee shall be non-binding, unless the Board of Trustees specifically agrees otherwise, but their content must be expressly considered where the decision is scientific or biomedical in nature.

### Article 22. Conflicts of interest

Every trustee must disclose any situation of conflict of interest, direct or indirect, real, potential or apparent, that may affect their independence of judgement.

The affected trustee shall abstain from participating in the corresponding deliberation and vote, save for a purely informational intervention authorised by the Board of Trustees. The Secretariat shall keep a record of the conflict and of the abstention.

### Article 23. Related-party transactions

Transactions with trustees, related persons, investee entities or significant funders shall require justification of need, economic reasonableness, market terms, the absence of preferential treatment and compliance with any required authorisations or notifications.

The Board of Trustees shall keep an internal register of related-party transactions, abstentions and decisions adopted.

### Article 24. Acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests and restricted-purpose funds

The Board of Trustees shall approve the criteria for accepting donations, sponsorships, bequests and restricted-purpose funds, ensuring that they do not compromise scientific independence, institutional reputation, legality, transparency or the proper application of resources to the Foundation's purposes.

Contributions shall not be accepted where they impose conditions incompatible with the Foundation's purposes, seek to predetermine scientific results or beneficiaries without evaluation, come from unlawful or insufficiently substantiated sources, or generate a significant reputational risk.

### Article 25. Selection of beneficiaries and projects

The selection of beneficiaries, projects, scholarships, grants or agreements shall be carried out in accordance with objective, traceable criteria appropriate to the Foundation's purposes. In biomedical research, particular attention shall be paid to scientific quality, feasibility, potential impact, ethical compliance, data protection, budgetary appropriateness and the absence of conflicts of interest.

### Article 26. Financial information and budgetary control

The Board of Trustees shall receive periodic information on the Foundation's asset position, budget execution, treasury, commitments assumed, donations received, restricted-purpose funds, funded projects and significant risks.

The financial information must make it possible to verify that resources are applied to the Foundation's purposes and that overhead costs are proportionate to the Foundation's size.

### Article 27. Accounts, activity report and audit

The Board of Trustees shall prepare and approve the annual accounts, financial report and activity report on the legally established terms. An audit shall be carried out where mandatory or where the Board of Trustees voluntarily so agrees for reasons of transparency, volume of funds, the nature of donors or the complexity of the projects.

### Article 28. Transparency and institutional communication

The Board of Trustees shall approve the basic guidelines on transparency and institutional communication. The Foundation may publish, in proportion to its size, the Bylaws, the composition of its bodies, the Code of Good Governance, the activity report, the accounts, the main funded projects and the general criteria for granting funding.

Public communication of scientific projects must be prudent, truthful, comprehensible and must avoid unsubstantiated therapeutic expectations.

### Article 29. Data protection and confidentiality

Trustees must maintain confidentiality regarding scientific, clinical, genetic, economic, contractual, personal or strategic information known to them by reason of their office.

Where the Foundation processes health data, genetic data, information relating to patients or families, biological samples or scientific records, the Board of Trustees shall ensure the adoption of reinforced safeguards for data protection, security, minimisation, consent, confidentiality and ethical evaluation where applicable.

### Article 30. Prevention of reputational risks

Before approving projects, alliances, public campaigns, significant donations or sponsorships, the Board of Trustees shall assess the potential reputational impact for the Foundation, particularly where these are linked to companies, activities, public controversies, patient data, rare diseases or treatment expectations.

### Article 31. Relations with patients and families

The Board of Trustees shall ensure that the participation of patients and families in the Foundation's activities takes place with respect for their dignity, privacy, autonomy, image, personal data and situation of particular vulnerability.

Fundraising or awareness campaigns must avoid the unnecessary exposure of minors, health data or identifiable genetic information.

### Article 32. Periodic evaluation of the Foundation's governance

The Board of Trustees shall carry out, at least every two years, an internal review of its operation, composition, profile needs, compliance with the Code of Good Governance, the quality of prior documentation, the follow-up of resolutions and the relationship with the Scientific Advisory Committee.

### Article 33. Review of the Regulations

These Regulations shall be reviewed where warranted by the Foundation's evolution, operating experience, amendment of the Bylaws, significant regulatory changes or a well-founded recommendation from the Board of Trustees, the Secretariat or the body responsible for compliance.

### Article 34. Interpretation and precedence

These Regulations shall be interpreted in coordination with the Bylaws, the Code of Good Governance, the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations and the Foundation's internal policies. In the event of contradiction, the law and the Bylaws shall prevail.

### Article 35. Entry into force

These Regulations shall enter into force upon their approval by the Board of Trustees and shall be applicable to all trustees from their acceptance of office or from the approval of the Regulations, whichever is later.
