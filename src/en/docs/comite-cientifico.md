## Preamble

The scientific credibility of a biomedical foundation depends not only on its purposes, but on the quality and independence of its processes for evaluating, selecting, monitoring and communicating projects. The Scientific Advisory Committee is the internal instrument intended to bring technical soundness, independence and traceability to the Board of Trustees' scientific decisions.

This Regulation governs the composition, functions and operation of the Foundation's Scientific Advisory Committee. The Committee does not replace accredited ethics committees, health authorities, universities, hospitals or research institutes; rather, it advises the Foundation internally so that its decisions are scientifically justified and consistent with the Foundation's purposes.

### Article 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Regulation is to govern the nature, composition, functions, independence, operating rules, conflicts of interest, confidentiality and issuance of reports of the Foundation's Scientific Advisory Committee.

### Article 2. Nature

The Scientific Advisory Committee is a collegiate, advisory, technical and independent body, with no executive or representative functions on behalf of the Foundation, except by express delegation from the Board of Trustees.

Its reports shall be non-binding, without prejudice to the special weight the Board of Trustees must give them in decisions of a scientific, biomedical, genetic, clinical, diagnostic, outreach-related or research-funding nature.

### Article 3. Organisational dependence and technical autonomy

The Committee shall be appointed by the Board of Trustees and shall be organisationally dependent on it, but shall act with technical autonomy in evaluating projects, formulating recommendations, assessing scientific priorities and issuing reports.

### Article 4. General functions

The Committee's functions shall include, among others:

- Advising the Board of Trustees on the Foundation's scientific strategy.
- Proposing priority lines of research into ataxias, related neurodegenerative diseases, genetics, genomics, molecular diagnostics, biomarkers, therapies, clinical trials, observational studies and personalised medicine.
- Evaluating research projects that request funding from the Foundation.
- Reviewing calls for grants, scholarships or research contracts.
- Proposing scientific, ethical, methodological, budgetary and impact evaluation criteria.
- Assessing the scientific suitability of agreements with universities, hospitals, research institutes, biobanks or public and private entities.
- Issuing reports on the scientific results of funded projects.
- Recommending measures for data protection, confidentiality, bioethics and scientific integrity.
- Proposing the involvement of external experts where the specialty of a project so requires.
- Informing the Board of Trustees of relevant scientific trends, advances and opportunities.

### Article 5. Functions in calls and grants

In calls for grants, scholarships, contracts or project funding, the Committee may take part in defining the scientific terms of reference, setting evaluation criteria, reviewing applications, proposing scores, identifying conflicts of interest, recommending awards and monitoring milestones.

### Article 6. Functions in research agreements

Before the signing of significant scientific agreements, the Committee may report on their scientific relevance, the strength of the research team, feasibility, budgetary consistency, technical risks, ethical review requirements, personal data, biological samples, and intellectual or industrial property linked to the results.

### Article 7. Limitations

The Committee shall have no executive, contracting, external representation, health-authorisation, ethical-approval or fund-disposal powers. Nor may it replace the opinions of research ethics committees, ethics committees for research involving medicinal products, health authorities, hospitals, universities or competent regulatory bodies.

### Article 8. Composition

The Committee shall be made up of a minimum of three and a maximum of seven members, appointed by the Board of Trustees from among persons of recognised standing in neurology, genetics, biomedicine, bioinformatics, clinical research, molecular diagnostics, rare diseases, advanced therapies, clinical trials, epidemiology, bioethics or other disciplines related to the Foundation's purposes.

Where the Foundation's growth so warrants, the Board of Trustees may increase the maximum number of members by amending this Regulation.

### Article 9. Recommended profiles

The Committee shall seek to include, at least progressively, the following profiles:

- A clinical specialist in neurology or paediatric neurology.
- A specialist in clinical genetics, genomics or molecular diagnostics.
- A researcher in translational biomedicine or advanced therapies.
- A profile with experience in bioinformatics, registries or data analysis.
- A profile with experience in clinical trials, observational studies or research methodology.
- A profile with sensitivity to, or experience in, rare diseases, patient involvement or bioethics.

### Article 10. Appointment criteria

Members shall be appointed on the basis of scientific excellence, clinical or research experience, independence, absence of structural conflict, complementarity of profiles, professional reputation, effective availability, and a reasonable balance of gender and institutional background.

### Article 11. Appointment

Committee members shall be appointed by resolution of the Board of Trustees, following an assessment of their profile, express acceptance of the position, an initial declaration of interests and a confidentiality undertaking.

The appointment resolution shall state the term of office, functions, the unpaid nature of the position, the start date and, where applicable, the specialty for which the member is appointed.

### Article 12. Term and renewal

Committee members shall be appointed for a term of four years and may be re-elected for successive terms. The Board of Trustees may agree partial renewals to ensure institutional continuity and scientific renewal.

### Article 13. Termination

Committee members shall cease to hold office upon expiry of their term, resignation, death, incapacity, loss of independence, serious breach of their obligations, an unmanageable structural conflict of interest, repeated failure to attend meetings, or a reasoned resolution of the Board of Trustees.

### Article 14. Chair

The Committee shall elect a Chair from among its members by simple majority, unless the Board of Trustees agrees on another arrangement in the initial appointment.

The Chair shall coordinate the Committee's work, organise meetings, drive forward reports and act as the ordinary technical point of contact with the Board of Trustees.

### Article 15. Secretariat

The Secretariat of the Committee may be held by a Committee member or by the person designated by the Board of Trustees. If the Secretary is not a Committee member, they shall have voice but no vote.

The Secretariat shall prepare notices of meetings and keep custody of minutes, reports, declarations of interest, confidentiality undertakings and evaluation documentation.

### Article 16. Unpaid nature and expenses

Membership of the Committee shall be unpaid, without prejudice to reimbursement of reasonable, duly substantiated expenses incurred in attending meetings, evaluating projects or taking part in activities approved by the Foundation.

Any exceptional remuneration for specific technical work other than Committee membership shall require the prior approval of the Board of Trustees, justification, market-standard conditions and a conflict-of-interest analysis.

### Article 17. Meetings

The Committee shall meet at least once a year, and whenever requested by the Board of Trustees, the Chair of the Committee, or one third of its members.

Meetings may be held in person, by electronic means, or in a hybrid format, ensuring identification, deliberation and a documentary record.

### Article 18. Notice and documentation

Notice of meetings shall be sent at least seven calendar days in advance, save in a justified case of urgency, and shall include the agenda, supporting documentation, the projects to be evaluated, a declaration of possible conflicts and, where applicable, a proposed assignment of rapporteurs.

### Article 19. Quorum and adoption of recommendations

The Committee shall be validly constituted when a majority of its members are present or connected remotely. Recommendations shall be adopted by simple majority of the participating members not affected by a conflict of interest.

Where there is no consensus, the report may briefly record dissenting positions.

### Article 20. Minutes

Minutes shall be drawn up for each meeting, recording those in attendance, matters discussed, documentation reviewed, declared conflicts, abstentions, and the recommendations and reports approved.

Minutes shall be internal and confidential, save where the Board of Trustees decides to publish extracts or general conclusions.

### Article 21. Reports

The Committee's reports shall, where relevant, record the project or matter analysed, the documentation examined, the evaluation methodology, the criteria applied, the scientific assessment, ethical or regulatory risks, data protection, declared conflicts of interest and the final recommendation.

### Article 22. Evaluation of projects

In evaluating projects, the Committee shall consider, among other things, the following criteria:

- Scientific quality and originality.
- Relevance to ataxias, related neurodegenerative diseases, genetics or diagnostics.
- Technical and time feasibility.
- Experience and standing of the research team.
- Potential clinical, diagnostic, translational or social impact.
- Methodological and statistical adequacy.
- Budgetary adequacy and co-funding.
- Compliance with bioethical and regulatory requirements.
- Data protection, confidentiality and information security.
- Existence of the necessary authorisations, consents, insurance or approvals.
- Monitoring plan, milestones, indicators and responsible dissemination of results.

### Article 23. Scoring system

When the Foundation calls for competitive grants, the Committee may apply a scoring matrix with weightings approved by the Board of Trustees or set out in the call's terms of reference. The matrix may include scientific quality, impact, feasibility, team, budget, ethics, data, and alignment with the Foundation's purposes.

### Article 24. Rapporteurs and peer review

The Chair may assign internal rapporteurs for each project. Where the specialty so requires, the Committee may propose external experts for peer review, subject to prior signature of a confidentiality undertaking and a declaration of interests.

### Article 25. External experts

External experts shall not be members of the Committee unless expressly appointed as such. Their involvement shall be limited to the matter entrusted to them and shall be subject to confidentiality, independence, a declaration of conflicts, and respect for the Foundation's evaluation criteria.

### Article 26. Conflicts of interest

Committee members must declare any actual, potential or apparent conflict of interest in relation to projects, researchers, centres, companies, donors, sponsors or collaborating entities.

The member concerned shall abstain from taking part in the corresponding evaluation, deliberation and recommendation, except for an authorised and documented informative contribution.

### Article 27. Specific conflicts in research

Relevant conflicts shall include, among others, belonging to the applicant team, having had recent and substantial scientific collaboration, having a hierarchical, financial or contractual relationship with the applicant, taking part in a competing entity, holding intellectual property rights affected by the matter, or receiving funding linked to the project under evaluation.

### Article 28. Confidentiality

Committee members and external experts must maintain strict confidentiality regarding the scientific, technical, clinical, genetic, financial, personal or strategic information to which they have access, even after they cease to hold their functions.

They may not use such information for their own benefit or that of third parties, nor disclose it, without the express authorisation of the Foundation.

### Article 29. Data protection

The Committee shall access personal data only where necessary for its function and with adequate safeguards. As a general rule, evaluation shall be carried out using anonymised, pseudonymised or minimised information, avoiding access to data identifying patients or family members except where specifically justified.

### Article 30. Bioethics

Where a project requires the opinion of a research ethics committee, an ethics committee for research involving medicinal products, health authorisation or institutional approval, the Scientific Advisory Committee shall verify the need for such requirements, but shall not replace them.

A favourable recommendation from the Committee may be made conditional on obtaining the relevant opinions, authorisations or consents.

### Article 31. Monitoring of funded projects

The Committee may propose to the Board of Trustees a system for monitoring funded projects, including interim reports, milestones, scientific justification, deviations, results, publications, and continuation, suspension or closure of the project.

### Article 32. Reports to the Board of Trustees

The Committee shall submit its reports and recommendations to the Board of Trustees through the Chair of the Committee or the Secretariat. The Board of Trustees shall retain final decision-making authority and must record its reasons when it departs from a significant scientific recommendation.

### Article 33. External communication

Committee members shall not publicly represent the Foundation, nor communicate the Committee's results, positions or recommendations, except with the express authorisation of the Board of Trustees or the Chair of the Foundation.

### Article 34. Documentation and record-keeping

The Secretariat shall keep custody of notices of meetings, minutes, reports, evaluation matrices, declarations of interests, confidentiality undertakings and other relevant documentation for the period determined by the Foundation and applicable law.

### Article 35. Review of the Regulation

This Regulation shall be reviewed at least every three years, or earlier if warranted by the Foundation's development, regulatory changes, experience gained from calls for grants, recommendations of the Committee or the Board of Trustees, or other relevant circumstances.

### Article 36. Entry into force

This Regulation shall enter into force upon its approval by the Board of Trustees and shall apply to Committee members from the date of their appointment and acceptance.
