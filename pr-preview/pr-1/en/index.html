Foundation object

Fundación Martina was founded to respond to a profoundly human reality: that of people and families living with ataxias and other rare diseases of genetic, neurological or neurodegenerative origin. It takes its name from Martina, a ten-year-old girl living with a neurodegenerative ataxia for which no specific treatment currently exists, and was established with the primary aim of driving, promoting and funding medical, biomedical, genetic and clinical research into ataxias, movement disorders and rare diseases related to genetic variants and DNA mutations.

Alongside research, the Foundation's core focus, it also takes on a welfare role: accompanying, informing and supporting patients and their families, particularly when those affected are minors or in situations of vulnerability. To ensure scientific rigour and good governance, the founders approved the Articles, appointed the initial Board of Trustees, and established a Scientific Advisory Committee, grounding all of the Foundation's work in human dignity, solidarity, transparency and social responsibility.

Chapter I. General provisions

Article 1. Name, nature and legal personality

Under the name "Fundación Martina", or such other name as may ultimately be admitted by the competent Registry, a foundation of general interest is established, of a permanent nature and without profit-making purpose.

The Foundation shall be governed by the will of its founders as expressed in the Founding Charter and in the deed of incorporation, by these Articles, by the legislation on foundations of Galician interest and other applicable regulations, as well as by its internal good-governance rules validly approved.

The Foundation shall acquire legal personality upon its registration with the competent Foundations Registry and shall thereafter enjoy full legal capacity and capacity to act for the fulfilment of its founding purposes.

Article 2. Founding Charter

The Founding Charter forms part of the constitutive act of the Foundation and expresses the founders' will to permanently commit a patrimonial endowment to the fulfilment of purposes of general interest linked to medical, biomedical, genetic, genomic, clinical and healthcare research, including the study of DNA and its variants and mutations, in rare diseases, especially ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders and related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions.

These Articles must be interpreted in accordance with the Founding Charter, as an expression of the institutional, scientific, healthcare, welfare and social purpose that inspires the creation of the Foundation, with particular attention to promoting biomedical, genetic, genomic and healthcare research into ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders and related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions.

Article 3. Registered office

The Foundation's registered office is established at Calle Río Ulla, 10, 15179 Oleiros, province of A Coruña, Autonomous Community of Galicia.

The Board of Trustees may agree to change the registered office within the same municipality. Where the change exceeds that scope or entails an amendment to the Articles, the requirements, notifications and registrations that are legally required shall be observed.

Article 4. Territorial scope of activity

The Foundation shall principally carry out its activities in Galicia, maintaining its registered office and its main centre of foundational activity within that territory, without prejudice to its ability, for the better fulfilment of its purposes, to promote or participate in actions, projects, agreements, scientific networks, welfare programmes, outreach campaigns or collaborations at the national, European or international level.

The national or international projection of certain actions shall be instrumental to the Foundation's purposes of general interest and shall be especially appropriate in relation to biomedical research, rare diseases, ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders, genetics, diagnosis, advanced therapies, patient registries or collaboration with specialised scientific centres.

Article 5. Duration

The Foundation is established for an indefinite period, without prejudice to the grounds for dissolution provided for by law or under these Articles.

Chapter II. Purpose, founding aims, activities and beneficiaries

Article 6. Purpose and founding aims

  1. The purpose and aims of general interest of the Foundation are to drive, promote, finance and, where appropriate, carry out activities aimed at medical, biomedical, genetic and genomic research related to the study of DNA, its variants and mutations, and to ataxias, including spastic ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases and related neurological or neurodegenerative disorders, as well as to improving scientific, genetic, healthcare and social knowledge of these conditions.

  2. In order to fulfil this purpose and these aims, the Foundation may develop, promote, finance, support or carry out, directly or through collaboration with third parties, the following lines of action:

    • The promotion and funding of scientific, clinical, translational and epidemiological research into ataxias, rare diseases and movement disorders, including genetic and genomic research, the study of DNA and its variants and mutations, molecular diagnostics, biomarkers, therapies and clinical trials, through the award of grants, scholarships, contracts or other support instruments.
    • Collaboration and the execution of agreements with universities, hospitals, research centres, and public or private, national or international institutions, for the carrying out of research projects and programmes, data exchange, and the development of scientific infrastructure such as biobanks, registries or platforms, particularly when oriented toward ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders or related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions.
    • Scientific outreach and social awareness-raising through the organisation and funding of conferences, publications, educational materials and awareness campaigns, fostering knowledge, prevention and the fight against misinformation.
    • Support for initiatives to improve diagnosis and access to research, particularly in rare conditions, ataxias and movement disorders, including funding for multicentre studies, patient registries, assessment tools or similar actions, always in compliance with data-protection, bioethics and biomedical-research regulations.
    • The promotion of the participation of patients and families in research programmes and informational activities, particularly in the field of ataxias, movement disorders and rare diseases, fostering association-building, responsible support, and dialogue with the scientific and healthcare community.
    • Fundraising and patronage through donations, bequests, sponsorships, agreements, grants, corporate collaborations, earmarked funds, and any other lawful forms of funding intended for the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.

  3. All activities shall be carried out on a non-profit basis, with resources and results applied to the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes in the manner legally provided and subject to applicable regulations.

Article 7. Activities for the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes

In order to fulfil its purposes, the Foundation may carry out, fund, promote, collaborate in or participate in the following activities, among others:

  • Announcing, awarding and managing grants, scholarships, subsidies, prizes, contracts or research programmes in ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases, genetics, genomics, the study of DNA and its mutations, molecular diagnostics, biomarkers, therapies, clinical trials, observational studies, translational research and personalised medicine.
  • Promoting, funding or carrying out its own projects or projects in collaboration with hospitals, universities, health research institutes, scientific centres, biobanks, scientific societies, public administrations, private entities, patient associations and national or international organisations.
  • Driving DNA, genetic, genomic, clinical, epidemiological, diagnostic or therapeutic studies aimed at improving knowledge, diagnosis, follow-up, treatment and quality of life for affected people, with particular attention to ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders and related molecular, genetic, biological or clinical mechanisms.
  • Supporting the creation, maintenance or funding of registries, repositories, data platforms, biobanks, research networks or scientific infrastructure, including those aimed at the study of ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders and related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions, with full compliance with applicable regulations on data protection, biomedical research, bioethics, informed consent and confidentiality.
  • Organising, promoting or funding conferences, congresses, seminars, publications, scientific materials, training activities, awareness campaigns and rigorous outreach programmes on rare diseases, ataxias, movement disorders, genetics, neurology, diagnosis and health.
  • Promoting the informed participation of patients and families in outreach, scientific or guidance activities, always respecting their dignity, privacy, autonomy, image, personal data and special situation of vulnerability.
  • Facilitating, funding, channelling or promoting, directly where legally possible and, where appropriate, through agreements with authorised professionals, centres or entities, medical-healthcare, pharmacological, pharmaceutical, psychological, psychiatric, therapeutic, welfare and social support for affected people and their families, especially minors or people in situations of vulnerability affected by ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases or related conditions.
  • Guiding families to facilitate access to specialised resources, healthcare professionals, follow-up programmes, support organisations, public or private aid, and appropriate welfare services.
  • Raising resources, donations, bequests, grants, sponsorships, corporate collaborations, earmarked funds and any other lawful contributions for allocation to the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.
  • Carrying out ancillary or instrumental economic activities, provided they are compatible with the Foundation's purposes, with its non-profit nature, and with applicable regulations.

Welfare or support activities of a healthcare, pharmacological, psychological or psychiatric nature shall be carried out in full compliance with applicable regulations, without substituting for the competences of public health services, authorised healthcare centres or competent professionals, and preferably through agreements or arrangements with duly qualified entities and professionals where necessary.

Article 8. Beneficiaries

The following, among others, may be beneficiaries of the Foundation's activity, in accordance with the Foundation's purposes and the criteria approved by the Board of Trustees, provided they may be recipients of its activities, whether natural or legal persons:

  • Patients affected by ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases or related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions.
  • Affected minors and people in situations of special vulnerability due to illness, disability, dependency, age or family circumstances.
  • Families, carers and people close to affected individuals, in relation to information, guidance, companionship, support and participation activities.
  • Researchers, research groups, healthcare professionals, universities, hospitals, health research institutes, scientific centres, biobanks, scientific societies and specialised entities.
  • Patient associations, social organisations, public administrations, private institutions, foundations and organisations that collaborate toward similar purposes.
  • Society at large, in awareness-raising, outreach, training and scientific and healthcare knowledge-promotion activities.

Article 9. Criteria for the selection of beneficiaries, projects and grants

The selection of beneficiaries, projects, grants, scholarships, agreements or programmes shall be carried out in accordance with objective, transparent, traceable criteria appropriate to the Foundation's purposes.

In matters of biomedical or healthcare research, particular attention shall be paid to scientific quality, relevance to ataxias, movement disorders and rare diseases, technical and budgetary feasibility, the soundness of the research team, potential diagnostic, clinical, therapeutic or social impact, bioethical compliance, data protection, the absence of conflicts of interest, and alignment with the Foundation's priorities.

Trustees, founders and related persons may not be principal beneficiaries of the Foundation, except in legally permitted cases and with the necessary safeguards to ensure the absence of conflicts of interest, transparency, objectivity and full alignment with the Foundation's purposes.

Chapter III. Assets, endowment and financial regime

Article 10. Founding endowment

The Foundation's initial endowment amounts to 30,000 euros, contributed in the manner recorded in the deed of incorporation, and is permanently committed to the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.

The endowment must be adequate and sufficient for the initial fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes, being presumed sufficient when it reaches the legally established amount, and its adequacy must be justified when it is lower or when the nature of the contribution so requires, without prejudice to its subsequent increase through new contributions, donations, bequests, grants, returns, capitalisation of results, or any other lawfully acquired assets or rights.

Where the endowment consists of assets or rights other than money, the valuation and documentation legally required shall accompany it.

Article 11. Assets of the Foundation

The Foundation's assets shall comprise:

  • The initial founding endowment and its subsequent increases.
  • The assets, rights and resources it acquires by any lawful title.
  • Donations, inheritances, bequests, grants, sponsorships, agreements, earmarked funds or contributions accepted by the Board of Trustees.
  • The returns, fruits, income and revenue derived from its assets, activities, collaborations or own resources.
  • The intellectual and industrial property rights, data, materials, publications, tools or results that may belong to it under funded agreements or projects, always within the applicable legal framework.

Article 12. Application of assets and allocation of income and revenue

The Foundation's assets, income, revenue and resources shall be committed to the fulfilment of its purposes of general interest.

The Foundation shall effectively allocate to the fulfilment of its founding purposes at least seventy percent of the net income and revenue obtained, within the terms and timeframes provided for in applicable legislation. The remainder must be allocated to increasing the founding endowment or reserves, once the legally admissible administration expenses have been deducted.

Administration, structural, communication, fundraising and representation expenses must be reasonable, proportionate to the size of the Foundation, duly justified, and aimed at maximising the impact on biomedical research, diagnosis, scientific outreach, support for patients and families, and the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.

The Board of Trustees shall ensure that the application of resources is effective, efficient, traceable and consistent with the Founding Charter, these Articles and applicable regulations, especially in relation to earmarked donations, grants, scientific agreements, multi-year projects, research programmes, biobanks, patient registries, or actions affecting minors, health data or genetic data.

Article 13. Acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests and earmarked funds

The acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests, grants, agreements or earmarked funds shall be the responsibility of the Board of Trustees, in accordance with the Articles, the Board of Trustees' Regulations, the Code of Good Governance, and any internal policies that may be approved.

Contributions shall not be accepted where they compromise the Foundation's scientific or institutional independence, impose conditions incompatible with its purposes, seek to predetermine scientific results or beneficiaries without proper evaluation, are of unlawful or insufficiently established origin, or may give rise to significant reputational risk.

Donations or earmarked funds must be sufficiently documented and allow the monitoring of their application, without prejudice to legitimate confidentiality and to data-protection regulations.

Article 14. Economic activities

The Foundation may carry out ancillary, complementary or instrumental economic activities, provided they are related to its purposes or necessary for its funding and sustainability, in accordance with applicable regulations.

Under no circumstances shall profits, surpluses or assets be distributed among founders, trustees, donors, collaborators or related persons. Any positive result shall be allocated to the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes or to strengthening its assets, within the legally permitted terms.

Article 15. Financial year, accounting, accounts and audit

The financial year shall coincide with the calendar year, unless the Board of Trustees agrees otherwise within legally permitted terms.

The Foundation shall keep orderly accounts appropriate to its activity, with the legally required books and, in particular, a daybook and a book of inventories and annual accounts where applicable, kept separately where necessary for specific projects, earmarked donations, grants or agreements.

The Board of Trustees shall prepare and approve the annual accounts, the financial report, the activity report and the action plan within the legally established terms, approving the accounts within the legal period following the close of the financial year, submitting them to the Protectorate within the legally established period following their approval, and submitting the action plan for the following financial year within the legally established period.

An audit of accounts shall be carried out when legally mandatory or when the Board of Trustees voluntarily agrees to it for reasons of transparency, volume of resources, the nature of donors, the complexity of projects, or institutional expediency.

Chapter IV. Governance of the Foundation: the Board of Trustees

Article 16. Nature and powers of the Board of Trustees

The Board of Trustees is the governing, representative and administrative body of the Foundation. It is the responsibility of the Board of Trustees to fulfil the Foundation's purposes, to administer with diligence the assets and rights comprising the Foundation's patrimony, and to ensure the correct application of its resources.

The Board of Trustees shall always act in the interest of the Foundation and of its biomedical, genetic, genomic, healthcare, welfare and general-interest purposes, without subordination to personal, family, professional, economic, business, political or institutional interests unrelated to those purposes.

Article 17. Composition

The Board of Trustees shall be composed of a minimum of three members and of the maximum number that the founders or the Board of Trustees itself may determine, within the legally admissible limits.

The Board of Trustees shall have, at least, a Chair and a Secretary, and may also have a Vice-Chair, Deputy Secretary, and other members.

Both natural and legal persons may be trustees. Legal persons shall designate a natural person as their representative in accordance with legally established terms.

The composition of the Board of Trustees shall seek to combine closeness to the founding will, institutional soundness, independence of judgement, and profiles suited to the fulfilment of the Foundation's scientific, healthcare, economic, legal, welfare and fundraising purposes, seeking to include or consult profiles with knowledge of rare diseases, neurology, neuropaediatrics, genetics, biomedical research or scientific-project management as the Foundation's development warrants.

Article 17 bis. Chair

The Chair of the Board of Trustees shall hold the ordinary institutional representation of the Foundation and shall exercise the functions assigned by law, these Articles, the Board of Trustees' Regulations, and the resolutions validly adopted by the Board of Trustees.

The Chair shall, in particular:

  • Call meetings of the Board of Trustees.
  • Chair them, and order and direct their deliberations.
  • Grant or withdraw permission to speak in accordance with criteria of order and proper functioning.
  • Resolve ties by casting vote, unless otherwise provided by law or these Articles.
  • Execute or order the execution of the Board of Trustees' resolutions.
  • Sign the public or private documents necessary for their execution.
  • Institutionally represent the Foundation before natural or legal persons, entities, authorities, bodies, public administrations and, where appropriate, judicial or administrative bodies.

The Chair shall always act within the framework of the Board of Trustees' resolutions and may not substitute for the Board's non-delegable powers, nor for the authorisations, notifications or controls that fall to the Protectorate, the Foundations Registry, or any competent authorities.

Article 17 ter. Vice-Chair

The Board of Trustees may designate one or several Vice-Chairs, numbered ordinally where there is more than one, from among its members and by the procedure provided for in these Articles or in the Board of Trustees' Regulations.

The Vice-Chair shall, in order, replace the Chair in cases of vacancy, absence, illness, incapacity, abstention due to conflict of interest, or express delegation, and shall exercise such functions as may be entrusted by the Board of Trustees or by the Chair within the statutory framework.

While the office of Chair is vacant, and until the Board of Trustees designates the person to fill it, the relevant Vice-Chair shall assume its functions on an interim basis, without prejudice to the obligation to convene the Board of Trustees where necessary to fill the vacancy.

Article 17 quater. Secretary and Deputy Secretary

The office of Secretary may be held by a trustee or a non-trustee. In the latter case, the Secretary shall have a voice but no vote, unless applicable regulations require otherwise.

The Secretary shall be responsible for safeguarding the Foundation's documentation, preparing and preserving the minutes of the Board of Trustees, issuing certifications with the Chair's approval, keeping the minute book and other corporate documentation, and assisting the Chair in the proper calling, documentation and formalisation of resolutions.

The Board of Trustees may designate a Deputy Secretary, whether a trustee or not, to support the Secretary and replace them in cases of vacancy, absence, illness, incapacity, or express delegation.

Article 18. Acceptance of office

The office of trustee must be expressly accepted in the legally established manner and, where appropriate, notified to or registered with the competent Foundations Registry.

Acceptance of office shall entail the obligation to act with diligence, loyalty, independence, confidentiality, transparency, absence of conflict of interest, and full commitment to the Foundation's purposes.

Article 19. Term, renewal and removal

The term of office of trustees shall be four years, without prejudice to the possibility of renewal, or indefinite if so validly established in the deed of incorporation or in these Articles.

Trustees shall cease to hold office on the legally established grounds, through resignation, death, incapacity, dissolution of the legal person, expiry of their term, removal, serious breach of their duties, or resolution adopted in accordance with applicable regulations and these Articles.

Removal from office must be documented and, where appropriate, notified to or registered with the competent Foundations Registry.

Article 20. Gratuitousness of office, reimbursement of expenses, and contracting

Trustees shall discharge their office without remuneration, receiving no payment for the exercise of their functions as trustees.

They shall be entitled to reimbursement of duly justified expenses incurred in the discharge of their office, in accordance with criteria of austerity, proportionality, transparency and traceability.

The Foundation may only contract with trustees or related persons in legally permitted cases and under legally permitted conditions, with express approval, justification of need, market-consistent terms, absence of preferential treatment, and compliance with any required authorisations, notifications or accountability procedures, with documentary record kept of the conflict of interest, the abstention of the affected person, and the objective advantage or necessity for the Foundation.

Article 21. Powers of the Board of Trustees

In addition to the powers conferred by law and these Articles, the Board of Trustees shall exercise the following functions:

  • Ensuring the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.
  • Approving the Foundation's overall strategy and its scientific, welfare and institutional priorities.
  • Approving the annual action plan, internal budget, annual accounts, financial report and activity report.
  • Approving calls for grants, scholarships, research contracts, agreements, scientific projects and welfare programmes linked to the Foundation's purposes.
  • Approving the investment policy, asset management, and the acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests and earmarked funds.
  • Appointing and removing members of the Scientific Advisory Committee and approving its Regulations.
  • Approving the Board of Trustees' Regulations, the Code of Good Governance, and any other necessary internal policies.
  • Resolving significant conflicts of interest and authorising, where appropriate, related-party transactions.
  • Creating committees, working groups or advisory bodies.
  • Amending the Articles where appropriate.
  • Agreeing to the merger, dissolution and liquidation of the Foundation in the legally established terms.
  • Ensuring compliance with data-protection, biomedical-research, bioethics, transparency, tax, accounting and other applicable regulations.
  • Administering, acquiring, preserving, encumbering, disposing of and transferring the Foundation's assets and rights, subject to applicable regulations, to the Foundation's purposes, and to any authorisations or notifications that may be required.
  • Exercising overall inspection, oversight and guidance of the Foundation's activity, approving its management plans and periodic action programmes, and determining the general criteria for the distribution and application of available funds.
  • Appointing, dismissing and, where appropriate, setting the terms of the Foundation's management, technical, administrative or collaborating staff, and granting and revoking general or special powers of attorney.
  • Appearing and acting on the Foundation's behalf before all manner of persons, entities, authorities, bodies, public administrations, courts and tribunals, being able to make requests, claims, appeals, actions, objections, withdrawals, settlements or submissions to arbitration, directly or through representatives, lawyers or attorneys.
  • Carrying out the economic, financial, banking, contractual and documentary transactions necessary or convenient for the fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes, including opening and closing accounts, collections, payments, deposits, investments, and the signing of public or private documents.

The foregoing powers are illustrative in nature and shall be exercised without prejudice to the competences legally attributed to the Protectorate, the Foundations Registry, and any competent authorities.

Article 22. Meetings, notice, and prior documentation

The Board of Trustees shall meet at least twice a year, and in any case as often as necessary to approve accounts, the action plan, budgets, significant projects, and scientific or welfare follow-up decisions, and whenever convened by the Chair, requested by the number of trustees provided for by law or these Articles, or necessary for the proper management of the Foundation.

Notice shall be given in writing, including by email or any other means allowing proof of dispatch, with a minimum of ten days' notice, except in duly justified cases of urgency.

The notice shall include the agenda, the venue or connection method, the date, the time, and the documentation necessary to deliberate and adopt resolutions with sufficient knowledge of the matters concerned.

No prior notice shall be required, and the Board of Trustees shall be validly constituted to deal with any matter, when all its members are present or represented and unanimously agree to hold the meeting and to the corresponding agenda.

Meetings of the Board of Trustees may be held at the Foundation's registered office or at any other location, within or outside Galicia, when so indicated in the notice or agreed by all attending trustees.

When a decision concerns scientific projects, research grants, biomedical agreements, healthcare or pharmacological assistance, genetic data, genomic data, information on DNA and mutations, biological samples, patient registries or significant ethical questions, particularly in projects linked to ataxias, rare diseases, minors or vulnerable people, efforts shall be made to obtain a report from the Scientific Advisory Committee or sufficient independent technical opinion.

Article 23. In-person, remote or hybrid meetings

The Board of Trustees may meet in person, by remote means, or in hybrid format, provided that the identity of attendees, the continuity of communication, the possibility of deliberation, and the documentary record of resolutions adopted are guaranteed.

Article 24. Constitution, deliberation and adoption of resolutions

The Board of Trustees shall be validly constituted when a majority of its members, present or represented, attend the meeting, unless the law, these Articles or the Board of Trustees' Regulations require a reinforced quorum for particular matters.

Trustees may grant their representation to another trustee for a specific meeting, in writing and with specific instructions where they consider it appropriate. The Secretary shall keep a record of representations granted.

The Chair shall order the deliberation and shall seek to ensure that all trustees have a reasonable opportunity to participate. On scientific, technical, legal, economic, healthcare or particularly complex matters, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, external experts, legal advisers, auditors, project leads, or persons invited by reason of the subject matter may attend with a voice but no vote.

Resolutions shall be adopted by simple majority of the attending trustees, present or represented, unless the law, these Articles or the Board of Trustees' Regulations require a qualified majority for particular matters. The votes of those who must abstain due to conflict of interest shall not be counted as votes cast.

In the event of a tie, the Chair's vote shall be decisive, unless the law, these Articles or the Board of Trustees' Regulations establish another rule for the specific case.

Trustees affected by a real, potential or apparent conflict of interest must disclose it immediately and abstain from taking part in the deliberation, voting, execution or management of the affected matter, except for purely informational involvement authorised and documented by the Board of Trustees.

The Secretary shall record in the minutes those attending, representations, the essential deliberations, resolutions adopted, the outcome of votes, abstentions, conflicts of interest and, where applicable, dissenting votes.

Article 24 bis. Reinforced majorities and non-delegable powers

Resolutions concerning the following matters shall require the favourable vote of at least two thirds of the members of the Board of Trustees, unless a higher majority is required by law:

  • Amendment of the Articles.
  • Approval or amendment of the Board of Trustees' Regulations, the Code of Good Governance, the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations, and the essential documents of the internal regulatory system.
  • Appointment or removal of members of the Scientific Advisory Committee.
  • Acceptance of donations, bequests, sponsorships or conditional earmarked funds of significant amount or with potential reputational impact.
  • Approval of multi-year research projects or significant financial commitments.
  • Related-party transactions with trustees, founders, family members, related entities, significant funders, or persons subject to a conflict of interest.
  • Disposal, encumbrance or transfer of assets or rights forming part of the founding endowment or of particular relevance to the Foundation's purposes.
  • Granting of general powers of attorney.
  • Merger, demerger, dissolution, liquidation, or the destination of the resulting assets.

Under no circumstances shall the powers relating to the approval of the annual accounts, approval of the action plan, amendment of the Articles, merger, dissolution and liquidation be delegable, nor those requiring authorisation from the Protectorate or which are non-delegable under applicable regulations.

The Board of Trustees may delegate other powers or grant powers of attorney within legally permitted terms. Any delegation or power of attorney must be documented, specifying the powers, limits, duration and accountability arrangements, and must be notified or registered where required.

Article 25. Minutes and documentation of resolutions

Minutes of each meeting shall be drawn up by the Secretary, with the Chair's approval. The minutes shall record, at least, the date, venue or means of the meeting, attendees, agenda, documentation examined, resolutions adopted, the outcome of votes, abstentions due to conflicts of interest and, where applicable, dissenting votes.

Minutes may be approved at the same session or at the following one. The Secretary shall safeguard the minute book and the documentary evidence of resolutions.

If the Board of Trustees does not expressly designate the person or persons responsible for executing a resolution, its execution shall be understood to fall to the Chair, without prejudice to any delegations or powers of attorney that may be granted.

The Secretary and, where applicable, the Deputy Secretary, even though they may not hold the status of trustee, shall be empowered to certify resolutions and to raise them to public instrument when necessary or convenient for their validity, registration or execution.

Chapter V. Advisory bodies, good governance, and internal regulatory system

Article 26. Scientific Advisory Committee

The Foundation shall have a Scientific Advisory Committee as a collegiate, technical, consultative and independent body, intended to bring scientific soundness, technical autonomy and traceability to the scientific, biomedical, genetic, clinical, diagnostic, outreach or research-funding decisions adopted by the Board of Trustees, particularly in relation to ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders and related neurological or neurodegenerative conditions.

The Scientific Advisory Committee shall be organically dependent on the Board of Trustees, which shall appoint and remove its members, but shall act with technical autonomy in the evaluation of projects, the formulation of recommendations, the assessment of scientific priorities, and the issuance of reports.

The Committee shall be composed of a minimum of three and a maximum of seven members, appointed from among persons of recognised standing in neurology, neuropaediatrics, genetics, biomedicine, bioinformatics, clinical research, molecular diagnostics, rare diseases, advanced therapies, clinical trials, epidemiology, bioethics, hereditary ataxias, movement disorders, neurogenetics, molecular biology, or lines of research related to ataxias, rare diseases, movement disorders or other disciplines linked to the Foundation's purposes.

Its reports shall be non-binding, unless the Board of Trustees agrees otherwise for a specific call or project, but must be given special consideration in decisions of a scientific or biomedical nature. Where the Board of Trustees departs from a significant scientific recommendation, it must record its reasons in the corresponding minutes.

The composition, appointment, term, removal, operation, confidentiality, conflicts of interest, reports, project evaluation, and relationship with external experts shall be regulated in the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations approved by the Board of Trustees.

Article 27. Basic functions of the Scientific Advisory Committee

The Scientific Advisory Committee shall, under the terms of its Regulations, be responsible for, among other functions:

  • Advising the Board of Trustees on the Foundation's scientific strategy.
  • Proposing priority lines of research into ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases, genetics, genomics, the study of DNA and its mutations, molecular diagnostics, biomarkers, therapies, clinical trials, observational studies, and personalised medicine.
  • Evaluating research projects seeking funding or support from the Foundation.
  • Assessing calls for grants, scholarships, contracts, scientific agreements, and research programmes.
  • Issuing recommendations on scientific quality, feasibility, impact, budget, follow-up, bioethics, data protection, and expected results of projects, including specific assessment of risks, expectations and safeguards where minors, genetic data, genomic data, information on DNA and mutations, biological samples, or particularly vulnerable patients are involved.
  • Proposing the participation of external experts where the specialty of the project so requires.

Article 28. Other bodies, committees, or working groups

The Board of Trustees may create legal, economic, welfare, communications, fundraising, compliance, ethics or other committees, working groups or advisory bodies that it considers necessary for the better fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.

The creation, modification or abolition of standing bodies shall be carried out in accordance with applicable regulations, these Articles and, where appropriate, the notifications, authorisations or registrations required before the Protectorate or the competent Registry.

Article 29. Code of Good Governance

The Foundation shall have a Code of Good Governance approved by the Board of Trustees, applicable, as appropriate, to trustees, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, executives, employees, collaborators, volunteers, external advisers, evaluators, suppliers, donors, sponsors and collaborating entities that take a significant part in the Foundation's activity.

The Code of Good Governance shall develop the principles of general interest, non-profit nature, scientific independence, transparency, accountability, integrity, prevention of conflicts of interest, efficient use of resources, enhanced protection of health data, genetic data, genomic data, DNA and mutations, respect for patients and families, responsible scientific communication, and regulatory compliance.

Article 30. Board of Trustees' Regulations

The internal functioning of the Board of Trustees shall be developed through Board of Trustees' Regulations, approved by the governing body itself, which shall regulate notice of meetings, prior documentation, deliberation, adoption of resolutions, minutes, execution of decisions, delegations, conflicts of interest, related-party transactions, acceptance of donations, the relationship with the Scientific Advisory Committee, transparency, financial information, and monitoring of the Foundation's governance.

Article 31. Internal regulatory system

The Founding Charter, these Articles, the Board of Trustees' Regulations, the Code of Good Governance, the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations, and the internal policies approved from time to time by the Board of Trustees make up the Foundation's internal regulatory system.

All of these documents must be interpreted in a coordinated manner, in accordance with the Foundation's purposes and the principles of transparency, scientific independence, non-profit nature, efficient application of resources, data protection, bioethics, institutional integrity, protection of patients and families, and respect for the general interest.

In the event of contradiction, applicable legislation and these Articles shall prevail, without prejudice to the obligation to adapt the remaining internal documents accordingly.

Chapter VI. Transparency, integrity, data protection and bioethics

Article 32. Transparency and accountability

The Foundation shall publish or make available, in a manner proportionate to its size and legal obligations, information relating to its Articles, the composition of the Board of Trustees, the composition of the Scientific Advisory Committee where applicable, the Code of Good Governance, the annual activity report, the annual accounts, the main projects funded, the general criteria for awarding grants, and aggregate data on donations, sponsorships and earmarked funds, respecting legitimate confidentiality and data-protection regulations.

The annual report shall seek to reflect the activities carried out, resources applied, projects funded, results achieved, impact indicators, and the degree of fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes.

Article 33. Conflicts of interest and related-party transactions

Trustees, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, and other persons subject to the internal regulatory system must avoid situations in which their personal, family, professional, scientific, economic or institutional interests could interfere with the interests of the Foundation.

Where a real, potential or apparent conflict exists, it must be disclosed immediately, and the affected person shall abstain from taking part in the corresponding deliberation, evaluation, decision or management, except for purely informational involvement authorised and documented.

The Foundation shall maintain an internal register of declarations of interests, related-party transactions, abstentions, and significant decisions, under the terms established by the Code of Good Governance, the Board of Trustees' Regulations, and the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations.

Article 34. Data protection, confidentiality, and use of image

The Foundation shall adopt enhanced safeguards when it processes, funds or facilitates the processing of data relating to health, genetics, diagnosis, family members, patients, minors, disease registries, biological samples, biobanks, clinical studies, testimonials, images, or otherwise especially sensitive information, including genetic data, genomic data, and information on DNA, genetic variants, mutations and molecular findings not confined to a specific gene, as well as identifying information about minors or family members.

The principles of lawfulness, fairness, transparency, data minimisation, purpose limitation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity, confidentiality and proactive accountability shall apply, among others.

When the Foundation collects testimonials, images, videos, medical records, family data, or information concerning minors or patients, it must obtain the necessary authorisations and consents, use comprehensible language, and limit public exposure to what is strictly necessary for the legitimate purpose pursued.

Article 35. Bioethics and responsible research

Projects funded, promoted or supported by the Foundation must have, where appropriate, the authorisations, ethics-committee opinions, informed consents, insurance, confidentiality safeguards, and regulatory requirements applicable, especially where they involve research with health data, genetic data, minors, biological samples, biobanks, clinical trials, or multicentre collaboration.

The Foundation shall not substitute for legally competent ethics committees, nor shall it assume healthcare, pharmacological, scientific or welfare authorisation functions that fall to authorities, centres, accredited committees, or qualified professionals.

All communication concerning research, DNA studies and their mutations, advances, trials, diagnoses, therapies or treatments must be prudent, truthful, comprehensible and scientifically responsible, avoiding the generation of unfounded therapeutic expectations, promises of a cure, or confusion between preliminary research and available treatment, with special caution in public communications relating to minors, rare diseases, ataxias, or lines of research at an early stage.

Article 36. Relations with patients, families and minors

The participation of patients and families in the Foundation's activities shall take place with respect for their dignity, privacy, autonomy, image, personal data, and special situation of vulnerability.

Fundraising, communication or outreach campaigns must avoid the unnecessary exposure of minors, health data, genetic data, or identifiable information about patients or family members, ensuring that any reference to Martina or other minors preserves their dignity, privacy, image and consent within legally required terms.

The Foundation shall seek to ensure that its information, companionship or guidance activities do not generate dependency, care-related confusion, or expectations incompatible with the available scientific evidence.

Chapter VII. Amendment, merger, dissolution and liquidation

Article 37. Amendment of the Articles

The Board of Trustees may agree to amend these Articles when it is expedient for the better fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes, to adapt the Foundation to regulatory changes, or to improve its organisation and functioning.

Any amendment to the Articles shall comply with applicable regulations and shall require, where appropriate, notification, authorisation, approval or registration before the Protectorate and the competent Foundations Registry.

Article 38. Merger

The Foundation may merge with one or more other non-profit entities where this results in the better fulfilment of the Foundation's purposes and the legally established requirements are met.

The merger agreement must preserve, to the extent legally possible, the scientific, biomedical, healthcare, welfare and general-interest purpose that inspires the Foundation, especially its orientation toward ataxias, movement disorders, rare diseases, and responsible support for patients and families.

Article 39. Dissolution

The Foundation shall be dissolved on the grounds provided for in applicable regulations, due to impossibility of fulfilling its purposes, by resolution of the Board of Trustees in legally established cases, or by final judicial or administrative ruling where applicable.

The resolution of dissolution must be adopted with the required majorities and procedures, and shall be subject to the notifications, authorisations or registrations that are legally applicable.

Article 40. Liquidation and destination of assets

Once the Foundation is dissolved, it shall be liquidated by the Board of Trustees or by liquidators appointed in accordance with applicable regulations.

The resulting assets shall be allocated to non-profit entities pursuing similar purposes of general interest, preferably dedicated to biomedical, genetic, genomic or healthcare research, rare diseases, ataxias, movement disorders, neurodegenerative conditions, or welfare support for patients and families, and which are subject to a comparable tax regime where legally applicable.

The destination of the assets shall be determined by the Board of Trustees or by the liquidators, subject to the founding will, these Articles, applicable regulations, and the approval or oversight of the relevant Protectorate.

Chapter VIII. Applicable regulations and interpretation

Article 41. Applicable regulations

The Foundation shall be governed by the founding will expressed in the Founding Charter and in the deed of incorporation, by these Articles, by the legislation on foundations of Galician interest, by such basic or supplementary state regulations as may be applicable, by the tax regulations applicable to non-profit entities and patronage should the Foundation avail itself of that regime, and by the remaining civil, administrative, healthcare, biomedical, tax, accounting, data-protection, intellectual-property, transparency and compliance regulations applicable to its activity.

Article 42. Interpretation

Any doubts as to the interpretation of these Articles shall be resolved with regard to the founding will, the best achievement of the purposes of general interest, the protection of scientific independence, transparency, the protection of patients and families, the Foundation's founding focus on the study of DNA and its variants and mutations, and on ataxias, movement disorders and rare diseases, and with regard to compliance with applicable regulations.

The Foundation's internal rules must be applied consistently with these Articles and with the Founding Charter. The Board of Trustees shall promote their review and updating where the Foundation's development, regulatory changes, recommendations of the Scientific Advisory Committee, operational experience, or the requirements of good governance so advise.

These Articles are approved by the founders and incorporated into the Foundation's deed of incorporation, without prejudice to any adaptations that may prove necessary at the request of the authorising Notary, the Protectorate, or the competent Foundations Registry.

Preamble

The Foundation carries out or funds activities of general interest in a particularly sensitive field: biomedical research, genetics, rare diseases, ataxias, diagnosis, scientific outreach, and relations with patients and families. Public trust does not depend solely on charitable or scientific purpose, but on how funds are governed, projects are selected, results are communicated, personal data are managed, and scientific independence is preserved.

This Code sets out the values, principles, and standards of conduct that must govern the actions of the Foundation and of the persons connected to it. It does not replace legal obligations but rather develops them and raises the internal standard of integrity, transparency, and accountability.

Article 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Code of Good Governance is to establish the principles, values, and standards of conduct that must govern the organization, governance, management, and activity of the Foundation, ensuring that all of its resources are applied in a transparent, efficient, independent manner consistent with the Founding Charter, the Articles, and its research purposes in the biomedical, genetic, health, and general-interest fields, with particular attention to ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders associated with variants of the VPS13D gene, and related neurological or neurodegenerative rare diseases.

Article 2. Subjective scope of application

This Code shall apply, as relevant, to members of the Board of Trustees, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, executives, employees, collaborators, volunteers, external advisors, expert evaluators, suppliers, partner entities, donors, and sponsors who play a significant role in the Foundation's activity.

Article 3. Material scope

This Code shall apply to institutional, scientific, financial, contractual, and communications activity, fundraising, the granting of aid, relations with patients and families, data protection, use of image, public outreach, and collaboration with third parties.

Article 4. Internal regulatory framework

The Founding Charter, the Articles, the Board of Trustees Regulations, this Code, the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations, and the internal policies approved by the Board of Trustees together make up the Foundation's internal regulatory framework. All of these shall be interpreted in a coordinated manner and in accordance with the Foundation's purposes, with applicable law and the Articles prevailing in the event of any contradiction.

Article 5. Guiding principles

The Foundation shall act in accordance with the following principles:

  • General interest and absence of profit motive.
  • Scientific and institutional independence.
  • Transparency and accountability.
  • Integrity, loyalty, and prevention of conflicts of interest.
  • Efficiency, restraint, and proportionality in the use of resources.
  • Respect for the dignity, privacy, and autonomy of patients and families.
  • Enhanced protection of health data, genetic data, and other special categories of data.
  • Compliance with applicable regulations on biomedical research, clinical trials, bioethics, data protection, transparency, taxation, and anti-money-laundering rules, where applicable.
  • Truthful, prudent, and scientifically responsible public communication.

Article 6. Foundational interest and absence of private benefit

No person connected to the Foundation may use their position to obtain personal, family, professional, financial, or reputational advantages incompatible with the Foundation's purposes.

Any surplus, returns, or income the Foundation may obtain shall be applied in full to the fulfillment of its purposes, with no direct or indirect distribution among trustees, founders, donors, employees, or connected persons, except for legitimate, justified, and legally permissible consideration for services rendered.

Article 7. Scientific independence

The Foundation shall preserve the technical and scientific independence of its decisions, particularly in the selection of projects, grants, scholarships, agreements, publications, research programs, institutional alliances, and the communication of results.

Donors, sponsors, or collaborators may not unduly influence the Foundation's scientific criteria, the independent evaluation of projects, or the content of scientific reports or communications.

Article 8. Scientific integrity

The Foundation shall promote standards of scientific integrity, data accuracy, traceability of results, respect for authorship, prevention of plagiarism, proper preservation of scientific documentation, and responsible communication of advances, limitations, and uncertainties. In projects related to VPS13D, ataxias, movement disorders, genetic data, biological samples, biobanks, or clinical trials, the Foundation shall seek to obtain an evaluation or recommendation from the Scientific Advisory Committee or from suitable independent experts.

Article 9. Bioethics and protection of individuals

Projects funded, promoted, or supported by the Foundation must hold, where applicable, the necessary authorizations, ethics committee opinions, informed consents, insurance, confidentiality guarantees, and other required regulatory requirements.

The Foundation shall not substitute for legally competent ethics committees, nor shall it assume health or scientific authorization functions that fall to accredited authorities, centers, or committees.

Where the funded or promoted activity involves biological samples, biobanks, patient registries, genetic data, or particularly sensitive health data, the Foundation shall require the responsible center to demonstrate sufficient informed consent, ethical or regulatory authorization where applicable, a regime governing access to samples or data, traceability, and confidentiality guarantees.

Article 10. Health data and genetic data

The Foundation shall adopt enhanced safeguards when it processes, funds, or facilitates the processing of data relating to health, genetics, diagnosis, family members, patients, minors, disease registries, biological samples, biobanks, or clinical studies.

Among other principles, the Foundation shall apply lawfulness, fairness, transparency, data minimization, purpose limitation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity, confidentiality, and proactive accountability.

Before promoting or funding the processing of health data, genetic data, or data concerning minors, the Foundation shall, where applicable, document the position it assumes with respect to data protection — controller, joint controller, processor, funder, or mere collaborator — and shall require the necessary contracts, joint-controllership agreements, impact assessments, security measures, and authorizations.

Article 11. Consent, information, and use of image

Where the Foundation collects testimonials, images, videos, medical records, family data, or information about minors or patients, it shall obtain the appropriate authorizations and consents, use clear and understandable language, and limit public exposure to what is strictly necessary for the legitimate purpose pursued.

Article 12. Relations with patients and families

The Foundation may promote the participation of patients and families in informational, outreach, social, or scientific activities, always guaranteeing respect for their dignity, privacy, autonomy, situation of vulnerability, and their right not to be used instrumentally for fundraising or communication purposes.

Article 13. Responsible scientific communication

The Foundation shall avoid creating unfounded therapeutic expectations, promises of a cure, misleading simplifications, or messages that confuse preliminary research with an available treatment.

All communication regarding research, advances, trials, diagnoses, or therapies must be prudent, truthful, understandable, and, where necessary, reviewed by persons with scientific or medical expertise.

Article 14. Proactive transparency

The Foundation shall publish or make available, in a manner proportionate to its size and legal obligations, the following information:

  • The Articles currently in force.
  • The composition of the Board of Trustees and, where applicable, of the Scientific Advisory Committee.
  • The Code of Good Governance.
  • The annual activity report.
  • The annual action plan, where legally required or advisable for reasons of transparency.
  • The annual accounts and, where applicable, the audit report.
  • The main projects funded or promoted, with an aggregate indication of their purpose, status of execution, and expected impact, particularly where they relate to VPS13D, ataxias, movement disorders, or related rare diseases.
  • The general criteria for granting aid, scholarships, and subsidies.
  • Aggregate information on donations, sponsorships, and earmarked funds, as well as on their general application, respecting legitimate confidentiality, data protection law, and commitments validly made to donors or beneficiaries.

Article 15. Accountability

The Foundation shall render accounts to the competent Protectorate and Registry, to donors, collaborators, beneficiaries, and society at large, on the terms legally required and in accordance with voluntarily assumed commitments.

The annual report shall endeavor to include information on activities carried out, resources applied, projects funded, results achieved, impact indicators, and the degree to which the Foundation's purposes have been fulfilled.

Article 16. Allocation of funds

The Foundation's resources shall be allocated primarily to the fulfillment of its founding purposes and, in any case, in the percentage and under the conditions required by applicable law. Structural, representation, communication, fundraising, and administrative expenses must be reasonable, proportionate, and geared toward maximizing direct impact on research, diagnosis, scientific outreach, patient support, and programs related to VPS13D, ataxias, spastic ataxias, movement disorders, and related neurological or neurodegenerative rare diseases. Earmarked funds shall be applied in accordance with their accepted purpose, provided this is compatible with the Articles and the Founding Charter.

Article 17. Donations, sponsorships, bequests, and earmarked funds

The acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests, or earmarked funds must respect the Foundation's purposes, its independence, applicable law, and the principles of transparency, reputation, traceability, and effective application of funds.

  • Contributions of unlawful or insufficiently proven origin shall not be accepted.
  • Contributions that would compromise the Foundation's scientific or institutional independence shall not be accepted.
  • Conditions imposing preferential treatment, predetermined selection of beneficiaries without evaluation, or deviation from approved criteria shall not be accepted.
  • Contributions whose publicity could cause serious reputational harm or is incompatible with the Foundation's values shall not be accepted.
  • Earmarked donations must be documented in writing, identifying their purpose, conditions, time frame for application, and method of monitoring, and must allow for accounting and material traceability. Where the amount, origin, nature of the donor, or conditions of the contribution so advise, the Foundation shall carry out a reasonable check of lawfulness, reputation, absence of conflict of interest, and compatibility with scientific independence.

Article 18. Relations with donors and sponsors

The Foundation shall treat its donors and sponsors with transparency, respect, and truthfulness. It shall inform them of how their contributions are used on the agreed terms, without granting them powers of scientific direction or undue control over projects, researchers, or results.

Article 19. Fundraising

Fundraising campaigns must be truthful, proportionate, and respectful of patients and families, and must avoid emotionally abusive messages, unnecessary exposure of minors, or the use of health data without an adequate legal basis.

Article 20. Conflicts of interest

Every person subject to this Code must avoid situations in which their personal, family, professional, scientific, financial, or institutional interests could interfere with the interests of the Foundation.

Where a real, potential, or apparent conflict exists, it must be reported immediately, and the person affected shall refrain from taking part in the relevant deliberation, evaluation, decision, or management, except for authorized and documented purely informational participation. Such abstention must, in significant matters, be recorded in the corresponding minutes or decision file.

Article 21. Register of interests

The Foundation shall maintain an internal register of declarations of interest, related-party transactions, abstentions, and significant decisions of trustees, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, executives, external evaluators, and persons involved in significant decisions, in coordination with the Board of Trustees Regulations and the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations.

Declarations shall be updated at least annually and whenever a significant change occurs.

Article 22. Gifts, invitations, and hospitality

No gifts, invitations, favors, or courtesies that could influence, or appear to influence, the independent judgment of the Foundation or of the persons acting on its behalf may be accepted.

Courtesies of symbolic value may be accepted, provided they are socially appropriate, proportionate, and not linked to a specific decision.

Article 23. Procurement and suppliers

The procurement of goods or services must be carried out on the basis of need, quality, suitability, reasonable price, transparency, and the absence of conflicts of interest.

For significant contracts, the Foundation shall seek to obtain several offers or justify the choice of supplier, particularly where there is a relationship with trustees, donors, researchers, or connected persons.

Article 24. Agreements with research centers, hospitals, and universities

Scientific collaboration agreements must define their purpose, duration, obligations, budget, ownership and use of results, publications, data protection, confidentiality, the regime governing intellectual and industrial property, ethical evaluation, use of biological samples or genetic data where applicable, rules on access to registries or biobanks, monitoring mechanisms, acknowledgment of funding, and any eventual social return on results.

Article 25. Intellectual and industrial property

The Foundation shall seek to ensure that funded projects expressly regulate the ownership, use, exploitation, dissemination, and eventual social return of results, publications, databases, materials, developments, biomarkers, diagnostic tools, software, or inventions that may be generated.

Article 26. Publications and acknowledgment of funding

Projects funded by the Foundation must appropriately acknowledge the funding received in publications, communications, materials, or results, without prejudice to respect for scientific independence, editorial standards, and the confidentiality of protected data.

Article 27. Minors and vulnerable groups

Any activity affecting minors, persons with disabilities, patients, or vulnerable groups must be designed with special care, data minimization, sufficient authorization, understandable language, consent or assent where applicable, prior review of the use of image, and an approach of enhanced protection, avoiding their instrumental use in fundraising or communication campaigns.

Article 28. Volunteering and collaborators

Volunteers and collaborators must receive clear information about their duties, limits, confidentiality obligations, data processing, and standards of conduct. The Foundation shall prevent volunteers from taking on technical, health-related, scientific, or data-management functions for which they are not qualified or authorized.

Article 29. Use of the Foundation's resources and assets

The Foundation's resources, accounts, brand, materials, databases, digital channels, and other assets shall be used exclusively for the Foundation's purposes and in accordance with applicable internal authorizations.

Article 30. Social media and digital presence

The Foundation's digital presence must respect the principles of truthfulness, scientific prudence, data protection, respect for patients and families, appropriate use of image, and consistency with the Foundation's purposes.

Article 31. Channel for queries and incidents

The Foundation shall establish an internal channel, proportionate to its size, for raising queries, flagging risks, reporting conflicts of interest, related-party transactions, incidents concerning personal data, use of image, scientific communication, or the application of earmarked funds, and for reporting any incident relating to compliance with this Code.

Communications shall be handled with confidentiality, good faith, and a prohibition on retaliation against anyone who reports responsibly.

Article 32. Regulatory compliance

The Foundation shall act in accordance with applicable law on foundations, the tax regime for non-profit entities, patronage, accounting, data protection, biomedical research, clinical trials, anti-money-laundering, intellectual property, procurement, transparency, and any other rules applicable to its activity.

Article 33. Proactive accountability

Compliance with this Code shall not be limited to avoiding infringements; it shall also require documenting significant decisions, identifying risks, adopting preventive measures, periodically reviewing procedures, and retaining reasonable evidence of compliance.

Article 34. Monitoring of the Code

The Board of Trustees shall be responsible for overseeing compliance with this Code. It may entrust its monitoring to the Secretariat, to a good-governance committee, or to a compliance officer, depending on the Foundation's development and size.

Article 35. Consequences of non-compliance

Non-compliance with this Code may give rise, depending on the nature of the relationship, to a warning, a requirement to abstain, removal from office, termination of collaboration, referral to the Board of Trustees, contractual review, or the adoption of appropriate legal measures.

Article 36. Training and dissemination

The Foundation shall seek to disseminate this Code among trustees, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, employees, collaborators, and other relevant third parties. Where the activity requires it, it shall organize basic training sessions on conflicts of interest, data protection, confidentiality, responsible communication, and the management of earmarked funds.

Article 37. Periodic review

This Code shall be reviewed at least every three years, or sooner if warranted by regulatory changes, the Foundation's growth, practical experience, audit recommendations, amendments to the Founding Charter, the Articles, or the internal regulatory framework, the start of activities involving genetic data, biological samples, biobanks, or clinical trials, or significant incidents.

Article 38. Acceptance

Persons subject to this Code must expressly accept it where their relationship with the Foundation so warrants. Acceptance may be recorded in the minutes accepting appointment to office, in a contract, an agreement, a letter of engagement, a declaration of interests, or a separate document.

Article 39. Entry into force

This Code shall enter into force upon its approval by the Board of Trustees and shall remain in force until replaced or amended by express resolution.

The Code shall be interpreted in coordination with the Founding Charter and the Articles, preserving scientific priority in relation to VPS13D, the protection of patients and families, scientific independence, transparency, and the general interest.

Preamble

The scientific credibility of a biomedical foundation depends not only on its purposes, but on the quality and independence of its processes for evaluating, selecting, monitoring and communicating projects. The Scientific Advisory Committee is the internal instrument intended to bring technical soundness, independence and traceability to the Board of Trustees' scientific decisions.

This Regulation governs the composition, functions and operation of the Foundation's Scientific Advisory Committee. The Committee does not replace accredited ethics committees, health authorities, universities, hospitals or research institutes; rather, it advises the Foundation internally so that its decisions are scientifically justified and consistent with the Foundation's purposes.

Article 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Regulation is to govern the nature, composition, functions, independence, operating rules, conflicts of interest, confidentiality and issuance of reports of the Foundation's Scientific Advisory Committee.

Article 2. Nature

The Scientific Advisory Committee is a collegiate, advisory, technical and independent body, with no executive or representative functions on behalf of the Foundation, except by express delegation from the Board of Trustees.

Its reports shall be non-binding, without prejudice to the special weight the Board of Trustees must give them in decisions of a scientific, biomedical, genetic, clinical, diagnostic, outreach-related or research-funding nature.

Article 3. Organisational dependence and technical autonomy

The Committee shall be appointed by the Board of Trustees and shall be organisationally dependent on it, but shall act with technical autonomy in evaluating projects, formulating recommendations, assessing scientific priorities and issuing reports.

Article 4. General functions

The Committee's functions shall include, among others:

  • Advising the Board of Trustees on the Foundation's scientific strategy.
  • Proposing priority lines of research into ataxias, related neurodegenerative diseases, genetics, genomics, molecular diagnostics, biomarkers, therapies, clinical trials, observational studies and personalised medicine.
  • Evaluating research projects that request funding from the Foundation.
  • Reviewing calls for grants, scholarships or research contracts.
  • Proposing scientific, ethical, methodological, budgetary and impact evaluation criteria.
  • Assessing the scientific suitability of agreements with universities, hospitals, research institutes, biobanks or public and private entities.
  • Issuing reports on the scientific results of funded projects.
  • Recommending measures for data protection, confidentiality, bioethics and scientific integrity.
  • Proposing the involvement of external experts where the specialty of a project so requires.
  • Informing the Board of Trustees of relevant scientific trends, advances and opportunities.

Article 5. Functions in calls and grants

In calls for grants, scholarships, contracts or project funding, the Committee may take part in defining the scientific terms of reference, setting evaluation criteria, reviewing applications, proposing scores, identifying conflicts of interest, recommending awards and monitoring milestones.

Article 6. Functions in research agreements

Before the signing of significant scientific agreements, the Committee may report on their scientific relevance, the strength of the research team, feasibility, budgetary consistency, technical risks, ethical review requirements, personal data, biological samples, and intellectual or industrial property linked to the results.

Article 7. Limitations

The Committee shall have no executive, contracting, external representation, health-authorisation, ethical-approval or fund-disposal powers. Nor may it replace the opinions of research ethics committees, ethics committees for research involving medicinal products, health authorities, hospitals, universities or competent regulatory bodies.

Article 8. Composition

The Committee shall be made up of a minimum of three and a maximum of seven members, appointed by the Board of Trustees from among persons of recognised standing in neurology, genetics, biomedicine, bioinformatics, clinical research, molecular diagnostics, rare diseases, advanced therapies, clinical trials, epidemiology, bioethics or other disciplines related to the Foundation's purposes.

Where the Foundation's growth so warrants, the Board of Trustees may increase the maximum number of members by amending this Regulation.

Article 9. Recommended profiles

The Committee shall seek to include, at least progressively, the following profiles:

  • A clinical specialist in neurology or paediatric neurology.
  • A specialist in clinical genetics, genomics or molecular diagnostics.
  • A researcher in translational biomedicine or advanced therapies.
  • A profile with experience in bioinformatics, registries or data analysis.
  • A profile with experience in clinical trials, observational studies or research methodology.
  • A profile with sensitivity to, or experience in, rare diseases, patient involvement or bioethics.

Article 10. Appointment criteria

Members shall be appointed on the basis of scientific excellence, clinical or research experience, independence, absence of structural conflict, complementarity of profiles, professional reputation, effective availability, and a reasonable balance of gender and institutional background.

Article 11. Appointment

Committee members shall be appointed by resolution of the Board of Trustees, following an assessment of their profile, express acceptance of the position, an initial declaration of interests and a confidentiality undertaking.

The appointment resolution shall state the term of office, functions, the unpaid nature of the position, the start date and, where applicable, the specialty for which the member is appointed.

Article 12. Term and renewal

Committee members shall be appointed for a term of four years and may be re-elected for successive terms. The Board of Trustees may agree partial renewals to ensure institutional continuity and scientific renewal.

Article 13. Termination

Committee members shall cease to hold office upon expiry of their term, resignation, death, incapacity, loss of independence, serious breach of their obligations, an unmanageable structural conflict of interest, repeated failure to attend meetings, or a reasoned resolution of the Board of Trustees.

Article 14. Chair

The Committee shall elect a Chair from among its members by simple majority, unless the Board of Trustees agrees on another arrangement in the initial appointment.

The Chair shall coordinate the Committee's work, organise meetings, drive forward reports and act as the ordinary technical point of contact with the Board of Trustees.

Article 15. Secretariat

The Secretariat of the Committee may be held by a Committee member or by the person designated by the Board of Trustees. If the Secretary is not a Committee member, they shall have voice but no vote.

The Secretariat shall prepare notices of meetings and keep custody of minutes, reports, declarations of interest, confidentiality undertakings and evaluation documentation.

Article 16. Unpaid nature and expenses

Membership of the Committee shall be unpaid, without prejudice to reimbursement of reasonable, duly substantiated expenses incurred in attending meetings, evaluating projects or taking part in activities approved by the Foundation.

Any exceptional remuneration for specific technical work other than Committee membership shall require the prior approval of the Board of Trustees, justification, market-standard conditions and a conflict-of-interest analysis.

Article 17. Meetings

The Committee shall meet at least once a year, and whenever requested by the Board of Trustees, the Chair of the Committee, or one third of its members.

Meetings may be held in person, by electronic means, or in a hybrid format, ensuring identification, deliberation and a documentary record.

Article 18. Notice and documentation

Notice of meetings shall be sent at least seven calendar days in advance, save in a justified case of urgency, and shall include the agenda, supporting documentation, the projects to be evaluated, a declaration of possible conflicts and, where applicable, a proposed assignment of rapporteurs.

Article 19. Quorum and adoption of recommendations

The Committee shall be validly constituted when a majority of its members are present or connected remotely. Recommendations shall be adopted by simple majority of the participating members not affected by a conflict of interest.

Where there is no consensus, the report may briefly record dissenting positions.

Article 20. Minutes

Minutes shall be drawn up for each meeting, recording those in attendance, matters discussed, documentation reviewed, declared conflicts, abstentions, and the recommendations and reports approved.

Minutes shall be internal and confidential, save where the Board of Trustees decides to publish extracts or general conclusions.

Article 21. Reports

The Committee's reports shall, where relevant, record the project or matter analysed, the documentation examined, the evaluation methodology, the criteria applied, the scientific assessment, ethical or regulatory risks, data protection, declared conflicts of interest and the final recommendation.

Article 22. Evaluation of projects

In evaluating projects, the Committee shall consider, among other things, the following criteria:

  • Scientific quality and originality.
  • Relevance to ataxias, related neurodegenerative diseases, genetics or diagnostics.
  • Technical and time feasibility.
  • Experience and standing of the research team.
  • Potential clinical, diagnostic, translational or social impact.
  • Methodological and statistical adequacy.
  • Budgetary adequacy and co-funding.
  • Compliance with bioethical and regulatory requirements.
  • Data protection, confidentiality and information security.
  • Existence of the necessary authorisations, consents, insurance or approvals.
  • Monitoring plan, milestones, indicators and responsible dissemination of results.

Article 23. Scoring system

When the Foundation calls for competitive grants, the Committee may apply a scoring matrix with weightings approved by the Board of Trustees or set out in the call's terms of reference. The matrix may include scientific quality, impact, feasibility, team, budget, ethics, data, and alignment with the Foundation's purposes.

Article 24. Rapporteurs and peer review

The Chair may assign internal rapporteurs for each project. Where the specialty so requires, the Committee may propose external experts for peer review, subject to prior signature of a confidentiality undertaking and a declaration of interests.

Article 25. External experts

External experts shall not be members of the Committee unless expressly appointed as such. Their involvement shall be limited to the matter entrusted to them and shall be subject to confidentiality, independence, a declaration of conflicts, and respect for the Foundation's evaluation criteria.

Article 26. Conflicts of interest

Committee members must declare any actual, potential or apparent conflict of interest in relation to projects, researchers, centres, companies, donors, sponsors or collaborating entities.

The member concerned shall abstain from taking part in the corresponding evaluation, deliberation and recommendation, except for an authorised and documented informative contribution.

Article 27. Specific conflicts in research

Relevant conflicts shall include, among others, belonging to the applicant team, having had recent and substantial scientific collaboration, having a hierarchical, financial or contractual relationship with the applicant, taking part in a competing entity, holding intellectual property rights affected by the matter, or receiving funding linked to the project under evaluation.

Article 28. Confidentiality

Committee members and external experts must maintain strict confidentiality regarding the scientific, technical, clinical, genetic, financial, personal or strategic information to which they have access, even after they cease to hold their functions.

They may not use such information for their own benefit or that of third parties, nor disclose it, without the express authorisation of the Foundation.

Article 29. Data protection

The Committee shall access personal data only where necessary for its function and with adequate safeguards. As a general rule, evaluation shall be carried out using anonymised, pseudonymised or minimised information, avoiding access to data identifying patients or family members except where specifically justified.

Article 30. Bioethics

Where a project requires the opinion of a research ethics committee, an ethics committee for research involving medicinal products, health authorisation or institutional approval, the Scientific Advisory Committee shall verify the need for such requirements, but shall not replace them.

A favourable recommendation from the Committee may be made conditional on obtaining the relevant opinions, authorisations or consents.

Article 31. Monitoring of funded projects

The Committee may propose to the Board of Trustees a system for monitoring funded projects, including interim reports, milestones, scientific justification, deviations, results, publications, and continuation, suspension or closure of the project.

Article 32. Reports to the Board of Trustees

The Committee shall submit its reports and recommendations to the Board of Trustees through the Chair of the Committee or the Secretariat. The Board of Trustees shall retain final decision-making authority and must record its reasons when it departs from a significant scientific recommendation.

Article 33. External communication

Committee members shall not publicly represent the Foundation, nor communicate the Committee's results, positions or recommendations, except with the express authorisation of the Board of Trustees or the Chair of the Foundation.

Article 34. Documentation and record-keeping

The Secretariat shall keep custody of notices of meetings, minutes, reports, evaluation matrices, declarations of interests, confidentiality undertakings and other relevant documentation for the period determined by the Foundation and applicable law.

Article 35. Review of the Regulation

This Regulation shall be reviewed at least every three years, or earlier if warranted by the Foundation's development, regulatory changes, experience gained from calls for grants, recommendations of the Committee or the Board of Trustees, or other relevant circumstances.

Article 36. Entry into force

This Regulation shall enter into force upon its approval by the Board of Trustees and shall apply to Committee members from the date of their appointment and acceptance.

Preamble

The Board of Trustees is the Foundation's governing, representative and administrative body. In a foundation oriented towards biomedical, genetic and health research, its actions must combine closeness to the founding purpose with institutional rigour, independence of judgement, traceability of decisions, control of conflicts of interest and reinforced protection of the trust placed in the Foundation by donors, researchers, patients and families.

These Regulations develop the internal operating rules of the Board of Trustees and must be interpreted in coordination with the Articles, the Code of Good Governance, the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations and the internal policies approved by the Foundation. Their purpose is to provide the Foundation with procedures that are clear, proportionate and compatible with the entity's future growth.

Article 1. Purpose

These Regulations govern the organisation, operation, convening, deliberation, adoption of resolutions and documentation of the decisions of the Foundation's Board of Trustees, as well as the duties of its members, the conflict-of-interest regime and the relationship of the Board of Trustees with the advisory bodies and with the Foundation's internal policies.

Article 2. Nature and institutional position of the Board of Trustees

The Board of Trustees is the Foundation's governing, representative and administrative body and shall exercise its powers subject to the law, the Articles, these Regulations, the Code of Good Governance and the other internal documents approved by the Foundation.

The Board of Trustees shall at all times act in the interest of the Foundation and of its biomedical, genetic, health and general-interest purposes, without subordination to personal, family, professional, economic, business, political or institutional interests unrelated to those purposes.

Article 3. Principles of conduct

Members of the Board of Trustees must act in accordance with the principles of diligence, loyalty, independence of judgement, good faith, confidentiality, transparency, austerity, efficiency in the application of resources, prevention of conflicts of interest, scientific integrity and respect for the dignity of patients and families.

  • Priority of the founding purpose over any particular interest.
  • Effective application of resources to research, diagnosis, scientific dissemination and activities linked to the Foundation's purposes.
  • Respect for the guarantees of data protection, bioethics, informed consent and confidentiality where an activity may affect health data, genetic data or biological samples.
  • Traceability of significant decisions through minutes, reports and sufficient documentation.

Article 4. Essential functions of the Board of Trustees

The Board of Trustees, in addition to the functions provided for in law and in the Articles, is responsible for the following essential functions:

  • Approving the Foundation's general strategy and its scientific and institutional priorities.
  • Approving the annual action plan, the internal budget, the annual accounts, the financial report and the activity report.
  • Approving calls for grants, scholarships, research contracts, agreements and scientific programmes.
  • Approving the policy on the acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests and restricted-purpose funds.
  • Appointing and removing members of the Scientific Advisory Committee.
  • Approving the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations and any amendments thereto.
  • Resolving significant conflicts of interest and authorising, where applicable, related-party transactions.
  • Resolving on amendments to the Articles, and on the merger, dissolution or winding-up of the Foundation, under the legally established terms.
  • Ensuring that the Foundation's activity complies with the regulations on data protection, biomedical research, bioethics, transparency and regulatory compliance.

Article 5. Composition and recommended profiles

The Board of Trustees shall be composed of the number of members provided for in the Articles. As far as possible, its composition shall seek a balanced presence of foundational, legal, financial, healthcare, scientific, institutional, communications and fundraising profiles, appropriate to the Foundation's biomedical and research nature.

The inclusion of scientific or healthcare profiles on the Board of Trustees shall not replace the independent technical function of the Scientific Advisory Committee, unless expressly agreed and always subject to the conflict-of-interest regime.

Article 6. Acceptance, term, renewal and removal

Acceptance of the office of trustee shall be formalised in the manner legally established and shall entail the express acceptance of the Articles, of these Regulations and of the Code of Good Governance.

The term, renewal and removal of trustees shall be governed by the Articles and applicable legislation. Removal shall not release a trustee from the duties of confidentiality regarding information known during the exercise of the office.

Article 7. Gratuitousness and reimbursement of expenses

The office of trustee shall be unpaid, without prejudice to the right to reimbursement of duly justified expenses incurred in the performance of the office, in accordance with criteria of reasonableness, austerity and transparency.

The Foundation may contract with trustees or related persons only where legally permissible, duly justified, carried out on market terms, and in compliance with the applicable regime of authorisation, abstention and documentation.

Article 8. Duty of attendance, preparation and information

Trustees must attend meetings of the Board of Trustees duly prepared, examine the documentation provided, request any information they consider necessary and participate actively in the deliberation and oversight of the matters submitted for decision.

The Secretariat shall provide the documentation with sufficient notice, particularly regarding the approval of accounts, the activity report, scientific grants, research agreements, restricted-purpose donations, asset transactions, conflicts of interest and related-party transactions.

Article 9. Notice of meeting

The Board of Trustees shall be convened in writing, including by email or another electronic means allowing a record to be kept, with at least ten calendar days' notice, except for duly justified reasons of urgency.

The notice of meeting shall include the agenda, the venue or connection system, the date, the time and the documentation necessary to deliberate and adopt resolutions with sufficient knowledge of the matters concerned.

Article 10. Prior documentation

For matters of particular significance, an executive note shall be attached, where appropriate, identifying the matter, background, proposed decision, economic impact, legal, scientific or reputational risks, any conflict of interest and supporting documentation.

Where the decision concerns scientific or biomedical projects, efforts shall be made to include a report from the Scientific Advisory Committee or, at the very least, sufficient independent technical judgement.

Article 11. In-person, remote or hybrid meetings

The Board of Trustees may meet in person, by remote means or in hybrid format, provided that the identity of those attending, the continuity of communication, the possibility of deliberation and the documentary record of the resolutions adopted are guaranteed.

The Secretariat may retain evidence of attendance, connection, voting and documentation sent, in compliance with data protection regulations.

Article 12. Agenda

The agenda shall be set by the Chair with the assistance of the Secretariat. Any trustee may request the inclusion of additional items, giving reasons for their relevance to the Foundation, with sufficient notice for their incorporation and supporting documentation.

No resolutions may be adopted on matters not included in the agenda unless all trustees are present or represented and unanimously agree to address the matter.

Article 13. Constitution of the Board of Trustees

The Board of Trustees shall be validly constituted when the quorum requirements set out in the Articles or in law are met. In the absence of a specific statutory rule, the attendance of a majority of its members, present or represented, shall be sought.

The Secretariat shall keep a record of those attending, proxies, apologies, guests and any abstentions due to conflicts of interest.

Article 14. Deliberation

The Chair shall conduct the deliberation and shall ensure that all trustees have a reasonable opportunity to speak. On scientific, technical or particularly complex matters, members of the Scientific Advisory Committee, external experts, legal advisers, auditors or project leads may be invited to attend with a right to speak but not to vote.

Article 15. Adoption of resolutions

Resolutions shall be adopted by the majority provided for in the Articles or in law. In the absence of a specific provision, they shall be adopted by a simple majority of those attending, excluding from the vote those who must abstain due to a conflict of interest.

The Chair may promote the search for consensus on particularly sensitive matters, even where this is not legally required.

Article 16. Reinforced majorities

Resolutions relating to the following matters shall require a reinforced majority of at least two-thirds of the members of the Board of Trustees, unless the Articles or the law require a higher majority:

  • Amendment of the Articles.
  • Approval or amendment of these Regulations.
  • Approval or amendment of the Code of Good Governance.
  • Approval or amendment of the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations.
  • Appointment or removal of members of the Scientific Advisory Committee.
  • Acceptance of donations, bequests or conditional sponsorships of significant amount or with reputational impact.
  • Approval of multi-year research projects or significant financial commitments.
  • Related-party transactions involving trustees, family members, related entities or significant donors.
  • Merger, spin-off, dissolution or winding-up of the Foundation.
  • Disposal or encumbrance of assets of particular significance or linked to the endowment or to the Foundation's purposes.

Article 17. Dissenting vote and record in the minutes

Any trustee may request that the substance of their vote and, briefly, the essential reasons for their position be recorded in the minutes. Where a decision departs from a significant technical report of the Scientific Advisory Committee, it is recommended that the specific reasoning be placed on record.

Article 18. Minutes

Minutes of each meeting shall be drawn up by the Secretariat, with the approval of the Chair. The minutes shall record, at least, the date, place or means of holding the meeting, those attending, the agenda, the documentation examined, the resolutions adopted, the results of votes, abstentions due to conflicts of interest and, where applicable, dissenting votes.

Minutes may be approved at the meeting itself or at the following one. The Secretariat shall keep custody of the minute book and the documentary evidence of the resolutions.

Article 19. Implementation of resolutions

It shall fall to the Chair, the Secretariat, Management if one exists, or the person expressly empowered to do so, to implement the resolutions of the Board of Trustees on the terms approved.

Where a resolution requires execution before a notary, registration, notification to the Protectorate, administrative authorisation or the formalisation of a contract, the Secretariat shall follow up on the matter until it has been fully implemented.

Article 20. Delegations and powers of attorney

The Board of Trustees may delegate powers or grant powers of attorney on the terms legally permitted and within the limitations set out in the Articles and in applicable regulations.

Every delegation or power of attorney must be recorded in writing, specifying the powers granted, the limits, the duration and the accountability regime. Powers that may not be delegated by law or under the Articles may not be delegated.

Article 21. Relationship with the Scientific Advisory Committee

The Board of Trustees shall request a report from the Scientific Advisory Committee on decisions affecting scientific strategy, the granting of research funding, project evaluation, significant scientific agreements, biobanks, patient registries, genetic data, biological samples or participation in clinical trials or studies.

Reports of the Scientific Advisory Committee shall be non-binding, unless the Board of Trustees specifically agrees otherwise, but their content must be expressly considered where the decision is scientific or biomedical in nature.

Article 22. Conflicts of interest

Every trustee must disclose any situation of conflict of interest, direct or indirect, real, potential or apparent, that may affect their independence of judgement.

The affected trustee shall abstain from participating in the corresponding deliberation and vote, save for a purely informational intervention authorised by the Board of Trustees. The Secretariat shall keep a record of the conflict and of the abstention.

Article 23. Related-party transactions

Transactions with trustees, related persons, investee entities or significant funders shall require justification of need, economic reasonableness, market terms, the absence of preferential treatment and compliance with any required authorisations or notifications.

The Board of Trustees shall keep an internal register of related-party transactions, abstentions and decisions adopted.

Article 24. Acceptance of donations, sponsorships, bequests and restricted-purpose funds

The Board of Trustees shall approve the criteria for accepting donations, sponsorships, bequests and restricted-purpose funds, ensuring that they do not compromise scientific independence, institutional reputation, legality, transparency or the proper application of resources to the Foundation's purposes.

Contributions shall not be accepted where they impose conditions incompatible with the Foundation's purposes, seek to predetermine scientific results or beneficiaries without evaluation, come from unlawful or insufficiently substantiated sources, or generate a significant reputational risk.

Article 25. Selection of beneficiaries and projects

The selection of beneficiaries, projects, scholarships, grants or agreements shall be carried out in accordance with objective, traceable criteria appropriate to the Foundation's purposes. In biomedical research, particular attention shall be paid to scientific quality, feasibility, potential impact, ethical compliance, data protection, budgetary appropriateness and the absence of conflicts of interest.

Article 26. Financial information and budgetary control

The Board of Trustees shall receive periodic information on the Foundation's asset position, budget execution, treasury, commitments assumed, donations received, restricted-purpose funds, funded projects and significant risks.

The financial information must make it possible to verify that resources are applied to the Foundation's purposes and that overhead costs are proportionate to the Foundation's size.

Article 27. Accounts, activity report and audit

The Board of Trustees shall prepare and approve the annual accounts, financial report and activity report on the legally established terms. An audit shall be carried out where mandatory or where the Board of Trustees voluntarily so agrees for reasons of transparency, volume of funds, the nature of donors or the complexity of the projects.

Article 28. Transparency and institutional communication

The Board of Trustees shall approve the basic guidelines on transparency and institutional communication. The Foundation may publish, in proportion to its size, the Articles, the composition of its bodies, the Code of Good Governance, the activity report, the accounts, the main funded projects and the general criteria for granting funding.

Public communication of scientific projects must be prudent, truthful, comprehensible and must avoid unsubstantiated therapeutic expectations.

Article 29. Data protection and confidentiality

Trustees must maintain confidentiality regarding scientific, clinical, genetic, economic, contractual, personal or strategic information known to them by reason of their office.

Where the Foundation processes health data, genetic data, information relating to patients or families, biological samples or scientific records, the Board of Trustees shall ensure the adoption of reinforced safeguards for data protection, security, minimisation, consent, confidentiality and ethical evaluation where applicable.

Article 30. Prevention of reputational risks

Before approving projects, alliances, public campaigns, significant donations or sponsorships, the Board of Trustees shall assess the potential reputational impact for the Foundation, particularly where these are linked to companies, activities, public controversies, patient data, rare diseases or treatment expectations.

Article 31. Relations with patients and families

The Board of Trustees shall ensure that the participation of patients and families in the Foundation's activities takes place with respect for their dignity, privacy, autonomy, image, personal data and situation of particular vulnerability.

Fundraising or awareness campaigns must avoid the unnecessary exposure of minors, health data or identifiable genetic information.

Article 32. Periodic evaluation of the Foundation's governance

The Board of Trustees shall carry out, at least every two years, an internal review of its operation, composition, profile needs, compliance with the Code of Good Governance, the quality of prior documentation, the follow-up of resolutions and the relationship with the Scientific Advisory Committee.

Article 33. Review of the Regulations

These Regulations shall be reviewed where warranted by the Foundation's evolution, operating experience, amendment of the Articles, significant regulatory changes or a well-founded recommendation from the Board of Trustees, the Secretariat or the body responsible for compliance.

Article 34. Interpretation and precedence

These Regulations shall be interpreted in coordination with the Articles, the Code of Good Governance, the Scientific Advisory Committee Regulations and the Foundation's internal policies. In the event of contradiction, the law and the Articles shall prevail.

Article 35. Entry into force

These Regulations shall enter into force upon their approval by the Board of Trustees and shall be applicable to all trustees from their acceptance of office or from the approval of the Regulations, whichever is later.
